References

Every source the twelve chapters cite, merged into one list: 159 works from 207 numbered chapter entries. Each entry says which chapters cite it, with links to the exact item in that chapter's source list, and how well we checked it.

How this book was researched

Before any chapter was written, seven research passes ran in parallel, each with its own brief: foundations and hardware; Zig; Odin; Rust; a language-neutral catalogue of techniques; real-world case studies with measured results; and the visualisation and front-end design of the book itself. Each pass searched the web, opened the pages it relied on, and wrote a notes file with a SOURCES section that says which pages were opened and which were only seen in search results. Those notes are published unchanged apart from path scrubbing, in Research notes below.

Language claims were then checked against the toolchains and their source trees rather than against blog posts: every code sample in the book is copied from a file in code/ that compiles and runs with the versions listed in Language versions and source trees, and the three languages' versions of each program must print the same checksum. Stability attributes, default targets, allocator behaviour and standard-library doc comments were read in the local source trees (~/langsrc/rust, ~/langsrc/zig, ~/langsrc/odin). Third-party numbers are quoted with their context and marked as cited; our own numbers come from one 4-vCPU KVM guest on an Intel Xeon (Ice Lake-SP), described in code/results/machine.txt, and are noisy.

Each source carries one verification label, taken from the chapter's own source notes and, where those are silent, from the research notes' SOURCES sections. When neither records that a page was opened, it gets the weakest label, seen. Tools and libraries are labelled read because their files are in the book or were run while building it.

  • read Opened and checked against the claim the book makes. (42 sources)
  • seen Seen as a search result or excerpt only; not opened in full. (66 sources)
  • via research notes Taken from the research notes below; not re-opened when the chapter was written. (51 sources)

Duplicates were merged by normalising URLs (scheme, www., trailing slashes and punctuation, youtu.be links, index.html) and by treating mirrors, transcripts and slide copies of one talk as one work. Where chapters gave different dates or affiliations for the same work, one form was chosen and used here.

Research notes

The raw notes from the seven research passes, as Markdown. They are working notes, not polished prose: they record what was opened, what was only seen in search results, and what was compiled, and some of their numbers were later re-measured or corrected in the chapters. Absolute local paths have been replaced with ~/langsrc/… or repository-relative paths. research/README.md describes each file.

  • research/01-foundations-hardware.md: Foundations and hardware. Llopis, Acton, Fabian and Kelley; latency numbers, caches, prefetchers, TLBs and branch prediction.
  • research/02-zig.md: Zig. Zig 0.17.1 idioms checked against the compiler: MultiArrayList, allocators, std.Io, the vectorizer, and the Zig compiler's own data layout.
  • research/03-odin.md: Odin. Odin dev-2026-11 idioms checked against the compiler: #soa, the context and allocators, bit_set, core:simd.
  • research/04-rust.md: Rust. Rust 1.100 / edition 2024: layout and repr, generational arenas, SoA crates, autovectorization and std::simd status.
  • research/05-techniques.md: Techniques catalogue. Language-neutral catalogue: what each technique is, why the hardware rewards it, when not to use it, and its best sources.
  • research/06-case-studies.md: Case studies. Real-world rewrites with measured results (Zig, Carbon, TigerBeetle, Ghostty, Linux networking, ECS engines) and their context.
  • research/07-visualization.md: Visualisation and front end. How the book's pages are built: libraries and versions, shared assets, component APIs and the visual style.
