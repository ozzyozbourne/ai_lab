The B+tree of chapter 07, with one change: a node is a 4096-byte page in a file, and a "pointer" is a page number. Put keys into it and watch pages fill up, split and get linked. Text of the chapter: README.md. Second page: the buffer pool during an insert that splits.

1. A tree of pages

Every box is one page of the file, labelled with its page id and with how many of its bytes are in use. Page 0 is the metapage and is not drawn; page 1 is the root and stays the root. A page is full when its bytes run out, so a leaf with a few large values splits as early as a leaf with many small ones. The figure starts with an empty tree. Put next scenario key replays, one key at a time, the 24 puts that the chapter's Zig and Rust programs print (key n carries a value of 300 + 24·n bytes); or type your own key and value size. A new put always starts from the tree as it is after the put you are looking at has finished. Tick Predict to be asked whether the cell fits.
