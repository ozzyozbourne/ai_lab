A page is too full or too empty. Instead of looking at that page alone, pool its cells with the neighbours, cut the pool into pages again, and write the pages back. Depending on how many pages come out, the same routine is a split, a redistribution or a merge. Chapter text: README.md. Second page: the policies compared on 20,000 keys.

What to try

  • Press play. The figure starts with the fixed scenario of the chapter's programs: an append, a split shared between siblings, a redistribution that needs no new page, and a merge.
  • Set siblings to 1. The scenario is run again with that setting, up to the operation you were looking at. Every overflow now creates a page, and the pages end up emptier. That is the plain split of chapter 14.
  • Put, Delete and Append work on the tree that is on screen. Step to any point of the scenario and carry on from there with your own keys; the steps after that point are dropped.
  • Switch the append fast path off and put keys in ascending order (leave the key box empty and use Append). Watch how much work balancing does to arrive at a worse result.
  • Go to the last step of the scenario, choose merge: at empty and delete keys. No cell ever changes page. Then try below 1/2.
  • Tick Predict to be asked how many pages will come out before the cut is shown.

A cell is drawn as wide as its byte size; a page holds 100 bytes. The parent holds divider cells (“keys ≤ 35 live in p0”) and one right-most pointer.
