How to read this

The chip runs one program that a compiler scheduled in full in advance; nothing is decided while it runs. Time runs left to right, one column per clock cycle. The upper rows are the units (memory slices, vector ALUs, matrix and switch units), one row per instruction queue; a box is an instruction dispatched on that cycle, labelled with its mnemonic. The lower rows are the stream lanes: data moves east (→) or west (←) on numbered streams, one position per cycle, and a box shows that the lane holds a value somewhere on the chip on that cycle (the number in front of a tag is how many values it holds at once). Colours group everything that belongs to one compiler tile; a faded box is a value that every reader has already used but that keeps flowing until it leaves the chip or is overwritten. The striped band at the left is the start-up Sync/Notify barrier, during which nothing happens. Hover over a box for its details, and click a column (or use the left and right arrow keys) to see the whole chip on that cycle in the space-time view below.
