An IVF index is a two-level tree: a root of centroids and one flat list of vectors per centroid. The root has no order, so routing is a nearest-centroid scan, and the true neighbours of a query can lie in a list that the scan ranked second. nprobe is how many lists you are willing to read.

1. One query, list by list

240 points in 8 lists. Diamonds are the centroids, numbered by list; a dashed cell holds every position that is nearer to its centroid than to any other. Click anywhere on the plane to place a query, or pick one of the 24 queries the chapter's programs run. Then change nprobe and compare with the exact answer.

2. Where the cells come from: k-means

Training alternates two steps until nothing changes: assign every point to its nearest centroid, then move every centroid to the mean of its points. Start with 8 random data points as centroids and step through.

What to take away

  • Routing is a search. A B-tree picks a child by binary search over sorted separators. Here the query is compared with all 8 centroids, because centroids have no order.
  • One child is not enough. A B-tree key falls in exactly one child. A query near a cell boundary has neighbours on both sides, so the index reads nprobe lists and the answer is still only probably right.
  • Recall is measured, not proved. The only way to know what was missed is to compare with a brute-force scan of all 240 points. With nprobe = 8 the index is that scan.
