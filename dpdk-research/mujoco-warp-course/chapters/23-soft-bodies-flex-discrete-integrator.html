    Part V · Chapter 23

    Soft Bodies: Flex, Cloth, Rods and the Discrete Integrator

    Ropes, cloth and squishy solids are not rigid bodies. MuJoCo models them as a flex: a cloud of point-mass vertices joined by edges and simplex elements, with elastic energy defined on those elements. This chapter takes MJWarp's flex machinery apart, from the Data arrays you read every step to the elasticity kernels, rods, interpolated flexes, flex collision and flex equalities. It then gives the full story of the DISCRETE integrator, which steps stiff soft bodies stably by solving in an effective metric, and maps every combination that mujoco-warp 4.0.1 refuses. It ends Part V with a checkpoint project: cloth on a box stack under a throughput budget.

    Expert About 90 min· Prerequisites: Ch 13, Ch 16, Ch 19, Ch 20

    You will learn

    • Build ropes (dim 1), cloth (dim 2) and soft solids (dim 3) with flexcomp and read the flex Data: flexvert_xpos, flexedge_length, flexedge_J.
    • What the elasticity kernels in passive.py compute: edge-based stretch, bending, stable Neo-Hookean (SNH), plasticity, discrete elastic rods, and interpolated (trilinear) flexes.
    • How flex contacts are found (collision_flex.py::flex_collision, MJ_FLEX_COLLISION_TABLE, candidates, de-duplication) and how FLEX and FLEXSTRAIN equalities fill the efc rows.
    • The DISCRETE integrator end to end: check_discrete, eff_build, eff_shift, eff_prec, eff_solve, regularize_constraint, and why it stays stable where Euler explodes.
    • Every unsupported flex combination in 4.0.1, and how to size buffers and pick solver settings for a cloth scene (Part V checkpoint).

    Before you start: recap

    • DISCRETE moves its work into forward: forward.py::discrete is one line, _advance(m, d, d.qacc) (Ch 13 §7). This chapter owns the details.
    • Passive forces (springs, dampers, gravity compensation, fluid) are summed in passive.passive during fwd_velocity; a plastic spring moves its own reference (Ch 12 §2).
    • All worlds share one flat contact pool d.contact.* of naconmax slots; nconmax is per world (Ch 9 §2).
    • make_constraint writes rows in the order equality | friction | limit | contact; each row has a type, an id, a Jacobian row, D and aref (Ch 19 §4).
    • CG and Newton minimize the same convex cost; Newton factors the Hessian, CG needs a preconditioner and more iterations (Ch 20 §5).
    • Sleep needs Newton and islands; some models are refused at put_model (Ch 21 §4).

    1What a flex is

    A rigid body has 6 degrees of freedom and one fixed shape. A soft body has a shape that changes, so MuJoCo describes it as a flex: a set of vertices, each attached to a body, joined by edges and grouped into elements. An element is a simplex of dimension dim: a segment (2 vertices) for dim=1, a triangle (3) for dim=2, a tetrahedron (4) for dim=3. Elastic energy, collision and rendering are all defined element by element.

    You rarely write vertices by hand. The MJCF macro flexcomp generates them: type="grid" with count="nx ny nz" and spacing builds a regular lattice, and the compiler also creates one body per vertex with three slide joints (x, y, z). A vertex is therefore a point mass with 3 DOFs, and a cloth with 36 free vertices adds nv = 108. A <pin id="..."/> attaches vertices to the parent body instead, so they get no DOFs of their own.

    Where flex work happens in a step

    Flex code is spread over the whole pipeline. The breadcrumb above highlights every station that has a flex part; this map says what that part is.

    Building flexes with flexcomp

    Three ways make a flex keep its shape, and they are very different for the solver: an edge equality adds constraint rows (§6), <elasticity> adds passive forces (§2), and <edge stiffness> (dim 1 only) adds a spring per edge. Play with the counts below to see how fast a flex grows.

    Pitfall: cloth elasticity is off by default

    For a dim=2 flex, <elasticity young=... thickness=...> alone gives no forces: the elastic2d attribute defaults to "none". Use "stretch" (membrane), "bend" or "both". We found this the hard way: without it the cloth of Example 23.2 simply fell away from its two pinned corners, with an edge strain of +61 (6100%). The C compiler also prints WARNING: flex 'cloth' is not rigid and has no equality constraints or passive forces to stderr for this model even with elastic2d="stretch"; the run above shows that the membrane forces are applied (MuJoCo C gives the same lowest z, 0.8509).

    The strain is the relative length change (L - L_0)/L_0. The cloth stretches by up to 9% near the pins, where the whole weight hangs, and compresses by up to 17% in the folds at the bottom: a membrane resists stretching but buckles freely, which is what cloth does.

    2Elasticity models and plasticity

    Recall from Ch 12

    Passive forces are summed in passive.passive during fwd_velocity into qfrc_spring and qfrc_damper; a plastic spring updates its own reference after the step (Ch 12 §2). Flex elasticity is one more term of that sum.

    MuJoCo has several elasticity models for flexes. They live in mujoco_warp/_src/passive.py, each as a kernel launched from passive.passive:

    ModeldimMJCFKernel (passive.py)Integrator

    stretch (membrane / volume)2, 3<elasticity young poisson>; dim 2 needs elastic2d="stretch" or "both"_flex_elasticityEuler, RK4 or DISCRETE
    bending2elastic2d="bend" or "both", thickness_flex_bendingEuler, RK4 or DISCRETE

    discrete elastic rod1rod bending / twist data (§3)_flex_rod_bending, eval_rod_chainsEuler, RK4 or DISCRETE

    The Integrator column means: an explicit integrator (Euler or RK4) with a small timestep, or DISCRETE; implicit and implicitfast are refused. In 4.0.1, forward.py::check_discrete raises for flex elasticity only under IMPLICIT and IMPLICITFAST (Euler and RK4 pass its checks), with the message "flex elasticity is no longer integrated implicitly under integrator='implicit' and 'implicitfast': set integrator='discrete', or 'Euler' with a small timestep". The stiffness of a flex is handled by the effective metric of §7, not by the implicit integrators' damping derivative.

    Stretch: energy in squared edge lengths

    For every element, _flex_elasticity (launch dim=(nworld, nflexelem)) works on its 3 edges (triangle) or 6 edges (tetrahedron). stretch_elongation measures each edge by the difference of squared lengths, a discrete Green strain:

    \varepsilon_e = L_e^2 - L_{0,e}^2 \;(- \text{plastic}_e), \qquad t = K_{el}\,\varepsilon, \qquad f_i \mathrel{-}= t_e\,(x_i - x_j)\ \text{for edge } e=(i,j)

    K_{el} is a symmetric 3×3 or 6×6 "metric" that the C compiler computes from young, poisson and the rest shape and stores in m.flex_stiffness (21 numbers per triangle element, the upper triangle of a 6×6; 24 per tetrahedron, FLEX_STIFFNESS_3D, where slots 21-23 hold the SNH flag/cubic coefficient, k_{22} and 1/\det D_m). stretch_metric unpacks it and stretch_tension multiplies. The energy is \tfrac14\varepsilon^\top K_{el}\varepsilon, a quartic in positions, which is the classic St. Venant-Kirchhoff (StVK) material written on edges. Damping is stiffness-proportional: kD = flex_damping / timestep and the damping elongation is built from flexedge_velocity, so a small-looking damping="0.001" is multiplied by the full stiffness. When flex_hessian builds the stiffness for DISCRETE it clamps negative (compressive) tensions to zero in stretch_stiffness, which keeps the matrix positive semidefinite.

    Stable Neo-Hookean and why it needs DISCRETE

    An edge-based StVK solid has a famous flaw: a tetrahedron turned inside out (reflected) has exactly the same edge lengths as the original, so it has zero energy and nothing pushes it back. flex_test.py::test_flex_3d_stvk_vs_snh_reflection checks precisely this. The stable Neo-Hookean model adds a cubic term (_snh_cubic, coefficient in slot 21 of the element's flex_stiffness block; a nonzero slot 21 is also how io.py's has_flex_snh detects SNH) and a volume term (coefficient k_{22} in slot 22, 1/\det D_m in slot 23): _snh_volume computes the volume ratio J of the deformed element and adds the pressure 2k_{22}(J - 1) along the volume gradient. Its Hessian is indefinite for inverted elements, so snh_project takes an SVD of the deformation gradient (_snh_svd) and projects the Hessian to be positive semidefinite. That Hessian only exists in the effective metric, so io.py raises ValueError("stable Neo-Hookean elasticity requires integrator='discrete'") for any other integrator (Example 23.5). SNH cannot be written to MJCF ("Stable Neo-Hookean elasticity is mjSpec-only"): set spec.flexes[i].elastic3d = 1 on a mujoco.MjSpec.

    Bending

    Cloth must resist folding, not only stretching. _flex_bending runs once per edge (dim=(nworld, nflexedge)) and skips boundary edges (flex_edgeflap[edge][1] == -1). An interior edge and its flap form a 4-vertex stencil. The compiler stores 17 numbers per edge in m.flex_bending: a 4×4 coefficient matrix that gives the linear force f_i = -\sum_j c_{ij} x_j (a discrete curvature operator, quadratic in positions), and a 17th coefficient that multiplies the triple-product terms cross(ed1, ed2) and so on for a curved rest shape. Damping uses the same matrix on the vertex velocities.

    Plasticity: a moving rest state

    Bend a paperclip and it stays bent. A flex gets the same behaviour from a numeric yield strain, <elasticity young="5e4" thickness="1e-3" elastic2d="both" yield="0.01"/> (MjSpec field stressyield, compiled to m.flex_yield, shape (nflex, 2)). yield takes one or two numbers, not keywords: entry 0 is the stretch yield and entry 1 the bend yield (types.py; _next_plastic_flex_stretch reads [0], _next_plastic_flex_bend reads [1]), and a single value fills both, so yield="0.01" gives [0.01, 0.01]. The compiler sets m.flex_plasticmode from elastic2d, as a bit mask (1 = bending, 2 = stretch) for the modes that are elastic and have a non-zero yield: our elastic2d="both" test cloth got mode 3, "stretch" gives 2, "bend" gives 1, yield="0" gives 0. It also reserves room in d.plastic from m.flex_plasticadr. The elastic kernels subtract the plastic part: that is the - plastic_in[...] in stretch_elongation and the extra term on c16 in _flex_bending. After every step, forward.py::_advance runs a return mapping: _next_plastic_flex_stretch (per element) computes an equivalent strain from the deviatoric strain and, when it exceeds the yield, moves the plastic reference by the factor 1 - \text{yield}/\varepsilon_{eq}; _next_plastic_flex_bend (per edge) and _next_plastic_flex_interp (per interpolation cell) do the same for bending and trilinear flexes. These launches only exist when m.nplastic > 0 and springs are not disabled.

    Passive flex contact (DISCRETE only)

    A flex with <contact passive="true"/> treats its contacts as stiff penalty springs inside the effective metric instead of as constraint rows. io.py sets m.has_flex_passive for non-rigid, non-interpolated flexes of dim ≥ 2 with that flag; _write_filtered_contacts then tags their contacts ContactType.PASSIVE, and passive.py::build_efm_contact builds a rank-1 metric term per contact (efm_con_dof, efm_con_scale, ...), applied by _eff_contact_force and _eff_contact_shift. It requires DISCRETE ("passive flex contact requires an integrator with the effective metric", from both the C compiler and check_discrete), and io.py refuses it for interpolated flexes: "Passive contact of interpolated flexes (dof=trilinear or quadratic) is not implemented."

    Version note

    The dev tree has a helper passive.flex_passive_contact(m) (dev tree only, not in 4.0.1). In the 4.0.1 wheel the same decision is the precomputed flag m.has_flex_passive; use that.

    3Rods: bending, twist and parallel transport

    A rope only keeps its length. A rod (a cable, a hair, a wire) also resists bending and twisting. A dim=1 flex with <elasticity young poisson> becomes a discrete elastic rod, the model of Bergou et al. 2008 that passive.py::_rod_hinge cites. Its energy has two parts:

    • Bending at every interior vertex (a hinge): with incoming edge e_0 and outgoing edge e_1, the curvature binormal is \kappa b = \dfrac{2\,e_0 \times e_1}{|e_0||e_1| + e_0\cdot e_1}, which is 2\tan(\varphi/2) times the unit binormal for a turning angle \varphi.
    • Twist along every edge: each edge carries a material frame (t, u, v), and the twist is how much u turns about t from one edge to the next, measured against a reference that does not twist.

    That twist-free reference is built by parallel transport: move the frame from tangent t_a to tangent t_b by the smallest rotation that maps one to the other. support.py::rod_parallel_transport does it in closed form:

    denom = 1.0 + wp.dot(t_from, t_to)
if denom < MJ_MINVAL:                      # antipodal tangents: no unique rotation
  return False, wp.vec3(0.0), wp.vec3(0.0)
u_raw = u_in - (wp.dot(t_to, u_in) / denom) * (t_from + t_to)
nu = wp.length(u_raw)
if nu < MJ_MINVAL:
  return False, wp.vec3(0.0), wp.vec3(0.0)
u_out = u_raw / nu
v_out = wp.cross(t_to, u_out)

    The figure runs exactly that formula along a rod whose centerline bends over time. Turn the twist slider to add a material twist on top of the transported frames.

    In MJWarp the rod pieces are:

    • io.py counts hinges and chain edges (_get_nflexrod_counts → m.nflexrodhinge, m.nflexrodedge) and m.flex_rod_has_twist; support.py::rod_has_twist reads it ("a 1D open DER chain with per-edge twist DOFs or box section").
    • smooth.flex launches _flex_rod_ref, which computes the per-world reference frames and invalidates a cache.
    • passive.py::eval_rod_chains evaluates every chain once per position state (hinge gradients, edge twist stiffness flex_rodedge_ktheta, end torques). Its docstring: "the first consumer (eff_build or passive) evaluates the chains and later calls return early".
    • _flex_rod_bending applies the forces; under DISCRETE, eff_build adds the rod stiffness (_eff_flex_rod_stiff) and eff_prec has a twist block solve (_eff_twist_solve).
    • forward.py::_advance launches _next_rod_twist, which carries the twist reference to the new configuration and stores it in d.plastic (that is why the rod below has nplastic = 48 without any plasticity).

    The elastic rod sags 6.5 cm and stays out to the side as a cantilever; the plain chain swings down around its clamp and is already past vertical on the other side (x = −0.42). Two details we learned while writing this example: one pinned vertex is only a ball joint, so a cantilever needs two (<pin id="0 1"/>), and for dim=1 the <elasticity> element adds bending and twist but not stretch, so the edge equality is still needed to keep the length. The compiler also gives the rod 6 extra twist joints (nv 24 instead of 18).

    4Interpolated flexes: a few nodes drive many vertices

    A dense soft body has many vertices, and each costs 3 DOFs. An interpolated flex (dof="trilinear", stored as m.flex_interp = 1) instead gives DOFs only to a coarse grid of nodes, the 8 corners of each cell, and moves every vertex as a blend of them. The shipped moving_base_strain.xml has 64 vertices but only 8 nodes: nv is 22, not 192.

    x_v = \sum_{n=0}^{7} \phi_n(s)\, x_n, \qquad \phi_n(s) = \phi(s_0, i)\,\phi(s_1, j)\,\phi(s_2, k), \quad \phi(s, 0) = 1-s,\ \phi(s, 1) = s

    That is support.py::eval_basis_trilinear (the node index encodes (i, j, k) in its bits). Forces and Jacobians go the other way: a contact on a vertex pulls on all 8 node bodies with those weights. add_interp_weight appends (body, weight) pairs and merges duplicates (up to MAX_INTERP_BODIES = 64), and support.contact_bodies_and_weights uses it for flex contacts.

    Elasticity of a trilinear cell is corotational: support.py::compute_interp_cell_quat computes the deformation gradient F at the cell centre from the node positions, divides by the rest size, and takes its rotation part by polar decomposition (mat33_to_quat_polar). Elastic forces are then computed in that rotated frame, so a rigid rotation of the cell costs nothing. The FLEXSTRAIN equality of §6 uses the same quaternion.

    Limits in 4.0.1 (all from io.py, Example 23.5):

    • dof="quadratic" (|flex_interp| == 2): "Quadratic flex interpolation (dof=quadratic) is not supported."
    • self-collision of an interpolated flex: "Self-collision of interpolated flexes (dof=trilinear or quadratic) is not implemented." (the default selfcollide is auto, so set selfcollide="none").
    • passive contact of an interpolated flex: "Passive contact of interpolated flexes ... is not implemented."
    • under DISCRETE + Newton, every node must sit on a 3-DOF slider body (m.flex_interp_assemblable), otherwise check_discrete asks for solver='CG'.

    A cloth drops on a sphere

    Read the rows: nefc starts at 85, one equality row per edge, before any contact exists. Each condim-3 contact then adds 4 pyramidal rows: at step 120 there are 120 contacts over 4 worlds, 30 per world, and 85 + 4 \cdot 30 = 205. The io.py defaults for any flex model are nconmax = 256 and njmax = 512 per world (256*has_flex, 512*has_flex in _default_nconmax/_default_njmax); we use 64/384, which the run shows is enough.

    Flex contacts in the pool

    A flex contact uses the same d.contact slots as a rigid one, with three extra vec2i fields, one entry per side: flex (flex id), elem (element id) and vert (vertex id), each -1 when that side is not that kind of object. On the flex side geom is -1. A plane contact touches a vertex; every other flex-geom contact touches an element. When m.nflex == 0 the three fields have shape (0,), so a custom kernel must never write them for a rigid-only model (Ch 26).

    Pitfall: classify contacts by geom, not by flex

    In 4.0.1 the rigid colliders write geom but never reset flex/elem/vert. A slot that held a flex contact in an earlier step keeps its old flex ids when a rigid contact takes it over: 16 of the 82 rigid contacts above carry a stale flex = [-1, 0]. The engine is not fooled, because support.contact_bodies_and_weights tests geom[side] >= 0 first. Do the same in your own code: a side is a flex exactly when contact.geom is -1 on that side.

    Pitfall: flex candidates also need room in naconmax

    The candidate arrays of the flex workspace are naconmax long and hold every hit before de-duplication, for all worlds. A cloth scene can therefore set the NARROWPHASE bit while nacon stays well below naconmax. In the checkpoint (Example 23.9) the peak was 28 contacts per world, the Ch 9 recipe gave nconmax = 48, and the run still overflowed until nconmax = 192. Size flex scenes from the overflow bit, not from nacon alone.

    Pitfall box (verified): what a flex model may not contain

    • any hfield geom: NotImplementedError: Flex-HField collision is not implemented. This holds even with contype="0" conaffinity="0" on the hfield. Model M7 TERRAIN can never be combined with a flex.
    • any SDF geom: NotImplementedError: Flex-SDF collision is not implemented.
    • a rigid flex (flexcomp rigid="true", all vertices on one body): NotImplementedError: Rigid flexes are not implemented.

    All three are raised by io.py::put_model, before any kernel runs (Example 23.5).

    A volumetric flex

    The block is 384 vertices on 1153 DOFs (the cylinder hinge is the last one), 1242 tetrahedra. It falls for 0.35 s, lands on the cylinder (contacts appear) and drapes. It runs under plain Euler at dt = 0.001 with young = 5e4: soft enough for an explicit step. The printed m.nefmK = 37998 is the number of nonzeros the stiffness matrix K would have under DISCRETE (§7); io.py computes it for every flex model from a temporary MuJoCo C solve (_get_efm_data).

    6Flex equalities: FLEX and FLEXSTRAIN rows

    Recall from Ch 19

    Rows are written in the order equality | friction | limit | contact; each equality kernel reserves its rows with wp.atomic_add(nefc_out, worldid, k) (Ch 19 §4). types.EqType lists FLEX ("couple the edge lengths of a flex") and FLEXSTRAIN ("strain constraint for interpolated flex").

    There are two flex equalities, both created by <edge equality="..."/>:

    • FLEX (equality="true"): one row per edge, holding its length at flexedge_length0. make_constraint launches _equality_flex_alloc (dim=(nworld, len(eq_flex_adr))) to reserve the rows, then _equality_flex with dim=(nworld, len(eq_flex_adr), nflexedge), one thread per edge. The Jacobian row is the edge's row of flexedge_J. The 6×6 cloth has 85 edges, so 85 rows before any contact.
    • FLEXSTRAIN (equality="strain", interpolated flexes, flex_edgeequality = 3): the compiler emits one FLEXSTRAIN equality per interpolation cell, and eq_data[0:3] holds that cell's (i, j, k). _equality_flexstrain (dim=(nworld, neq_flexstrain)) runs one thread per (world, equality), so each thread handles one cell. It reads neig, the number of stored deformation eigenmodes of that cell, from flex_stiffness, rotates the current node positions into the corotational frame of compute_interp_cell_quat, and writes neig rows, one per eigenmode. A model therefore adds about 18 × ncells rows. A trilinear cell has 3 \times 8 = 24 node DOFs; removing the 6 rigid-body modes leaves 18 rows, exactly what Example 23.7 measures.

    Edge equalities are cheap to write but not free to solve: every row is a constraint the solver must satisfy each step, and they are stiff (a length is either right or wrong). That is why cloth scenes have large nefc before any contact, and why njmax must include nflexedge per world.

    Recall from Ch 21

    Sleep needs Newton and islands (Ch 21 §4). Flex equalities add one more rule: io.py raises NotImplementedError("Flex equality constraints are not supported with sleeping enabled.") as soon as a model has an EqType.FLEX equality and the SLEEP flag. Under DISCRETE, check_discrete also refuses sleep with any flex stiffness ("Discrete integrator: sleep with flex not yet supported").

    7The DISCRETE integrator and the effective metric

    This chapter owns the topic

    DISCRETE solves with an effective metric built from passive stiffness (eff_build/eff_solve); Ch 23 §7. Ch 13 §7 gave the overview, Ch 19 §11 the different regularization, Ch 20 §12 the warmstart note; this section is the full story.

    Why a stiff body needs it

    Semi-implicit Euler (Ch 13 §3) updates v \leftarrow v + h\,a(q) and then q \leftarrow q + h\,v. For one spring of stiffness k and mass m, with \omega = \sqrt{k/m}, that map is stable only while \omega h \le 2. A cloth vertex weighs 2 g; a stiff membrane edge can easily have k in the thousands of N/m, so \omega is in the hundreds per second and h = 2 ms is already too long. Example 23.6 shows it: at young = 5e5 the Euler run turns to NaN within 20 steps.

    The fix is to evaluate the stiffness at the end of the step. Linearize the passive force around the current state, f(q + h v^+, v^+) \approx f - K h v^+ - D h a with v^+ = v + h a, and solve the momentum balance for a:

    \underbrace{(M + hD + h^2K)}_{\hat M}\, a = f - hKv, \qquad v^+ = v + h a, \qquad q^+ = q + h v^+

    \hat M is the effective metric. The force shift c = -hKv is what eff_shift's docstring calls "the smooth force shift c = -h*K*v". For the spring above the map becomes a = -(kx + hkv)/(m + h^2k), which is stable for every h. Try both below.

    Where the work happens

    The DISCRETE pieces are eff_build, eff_shift, eff_actuation and eff_solve in derivative.py, and regularize_constraint in constraint.py. The gating is in forward.py: each stage of _forward calls them inside if m.opt.integrator == IntegratorType.DISCRETE:. The first three also return early for other integrators; eff_solve and regularize_constraint have no integrator check of their own, so call them only under DISCRETE. Step through it:

    One step with integrator="discrete"

    forward.py::_forward + forward.py::discrete
    check_discrete(m)
fwd_position:  ...; eff_build(m, d)
fwd_velocity:  ...; eff_shift(m, d)
fwd_actuation: ...; eff_actuation(m, d)
               regularize_constraint(m, d)
fwd_acceleration: eff_solve(m, d, qacc_smooth)
solver.solve(m, d)  # in the metric
_launch_discrete_free_gyro(m, d, ...)
discrete(m, d): _advance(m, d, d.qacc)

    Inside eff_solve: preconditioned CG in the metric

    \hat M is never formed as a dense matrix. eff_mul_m applies it to a vector matrix-free (mass matrix, the CSR stiffness efm_K_val with the pattern m.efm_K_rownnz/rowadr/colind that io.py precomputes, tendon, actuator, rod-twist, passive-contact and interpolation terms), and eff_solve runs a preconditioned conjugate gradient with it. eff_prec is the preconditioner: it solves with the factored backbone qHLD, the factor of qH = M + metric diagonals that eff_actuation builds (the factor also gets the diagonal of K). If a model has no flex, tendon or actuator terms at all, the backbone is exact and eff_solve takes a fast path: one smooth.solve_LD and done.

    The PCG kernels are tiled, one block per world (wp.launch_tiled(..., dim=d.nworld, block_dim=m.block_dim.eff_pcg), dot products with wp.tile_reduce). They start from x_0 = 0, stop a world when |r|^2 < \max(\text{tolerance}, 10^{-5})^2\,|b|^2 and count unfinished worlds in efm_nsolving. With m.opt.graph_conditional on a GPU the loop is wp.capture_while(efm_nsolving, while_body=_pcg_iteration), so it runs on the device with no host round trip; otherwise it is a Python loop of m.opt.iterations iterations.

    PCG on a 3-vertex chain, numbers computed live

    derivative.py::eff_solveToy system
    z = eff_prec(b)        # P^-1 b
qacc = 0; r = b; p = z; rz = r.z
while efm_nsolving > 0:
  Ap = eff_mul_m(p)    # M_hat p
  alpha = rz / (p.Ap)
  qacc += alpha p; r -= alpha Ap
  done if r.r < tol^2 b.b
  z = eff_prec(r)
  beta = r.z / rz; p = z + beta p

    Constraints in the effective metric

    Two things change for constraints. First, _efc_row takes its is_discrete branch for the impedance (Ch 19 §11). Second, after actuation constraint.regularize_constraint (launch dim=(nworld, njmax)) rescales every row's D by how much the metric stiffens it, using only diagonals:

    s = \frac{\sum_c J_c^2 / qH_{cc}}{\sum_c J_c^2 / M_{cc}}, \qquad D \leftarrow \min(D / s,\ 1/\text{MJ\_MINVAL})

    A row that touches only stiff flex DOFs (large qH diagonal) gets a smaller s and a larger D. Contact rows use the Jacobian of their first row (efc_address[conid, 0]), and adhesion contacts also shift aref. The solver then minimizes the Ch 19 cost with \hat M in place of M: _solve replaces qfrc_smooth by qfrc_smooth + efm_c + efm_ca (_add_qfrc_smooth_eff), CG preconditions with eff_prec (and folds rod-twist terms with eff_prec_fold), and Newton adds the CSR stiffness into its Hessian (_add_efmK_metric_dense). Newton needs that assembly, which is why "discrete integrator: flex with general attachments requires solver='CG'": a flex whose vertices sit on bodies with general joints has no simple CSR K. After the solve, _launch_discrete_free_gyro corrects the gyroscopic term of free bodies, and discrete is just _advance(m, d, d.qacc).

    Version note

    C MuJoCo can start the solver from the cheaper of qacc_warmstart and qacc_smooth. The dev tree adds that for DISCRETE only (solver.py::_warmstart_best, dev tree only, not in 4.0.1, with a TODO to extend it). In 4.0.1 DISCRETE starts from qacc_warmstart like every other integrator (Ch 20 §12).

    Three lessons. At young = 5e5 Euler is non-finite within the first 20 steps while DISCRETE is fine. At young = 5e4 both are stable, but the DISCRETE cloth has 4x less peak strain after 0.8 s: the implicit step damps the fast stretch modes (the purple curve of Figure 10), which is usually what you want for cloth, but it is numerical damping, not material damping. And DISCRETE costs more per step (here about 3-4x on the CPU, because of eff_build and the PCG solve); it buys you a timestep that Euler cannot take at all. solver_niter is 1 because this model has no constraint rows.

    8What mujoco-warp 4.0.1 refuses

    The README calls flex support experimental, and the code is honest about its gaps: every unsupported combination raises a clear exception, either in io.py::put_model (before anything runs) or in forward.py::check_discrete, which is the first line of _forward and step1. Example 23.5 triggers each one.

    Read it as a decision list before you build a soft-body scene:

    1. Scene contents: no hfield, no SDF, no rigid flex, no body/actuator/sensor plugins, and no contact sensors that report linvel/angvel ("Contact sensor linvel and angvel not supported for models with flexes.").
    2. Interpolation: trilinear only; no self-collision or passive contact on it.
    3. Integrator: elasticity means an explicit integrator (Euler or RK4) with a small dt, or DISCRETE; SNH and passive contact mean DISCRETE; DISCRETE means no edge stiffness (use <elasticity> or an edge equality) and no noslip; DISCRETE + Newton means simple attachments (vertices on 3-slider bodies under the world) and assemblable interpolation nodes, otherwise use CG.
    4. Sleep: no flex edge equality (equality="true", EqType.FLEX), and under DISCRETE no flex stiffness, and islands on.

    9Rendering flexes

    The batch renderer of Ch 27 ray-casts every world at once against a BVH. Flexes enter that BVH in two ways (bvh.py):

    • a 1-D flex adds one capsule per edge to the scene BVH (flex_geom_edgeid ≥ 0);
    • a 2-D or 3-D flex adds one box per flex to the scene BVH plus its own triangle mesh, built by build_flex_bvh (_build_flex_2d_elements, _build_flex_2d_sides, _build_flex_3d_shells).

    Because the vertices move every step, bvh.refit_bvh calls refit_flex_bvh(m, d, rc) whenever m.nflex > 0: it recomputes vertex normals, moves the face points of a 2-D flex by ±flex_radius along the normal (so cloth has two faces and a thickness), and refits the mesh BVH. The flex_render_smooth argument of mjw.create_render_context (default True, stored on the RenderContext) decides which normal _update_flex_2d_face_points offsets along: the averaged vertex normals (smooth, the shell bends continuously) or each triangle's own normal (faceted). Segmentation reports flex pixels as (flex_id, mjOBJ_FLEX = 9).

    The renderer, its arguments and the BVH are taught in Ch 27; the notebook of this chapter renders one cloth frame with it on a GPU.

    10Part V checkpoint: cloth on a box stack under a throughput budget

    Part V taught the constraint problem (Ch 19), the solvers (Ch 20), islands and sleep (Ch 21), determinism (Ch 22) and now soft bodies. The checkpoint puts the knobs together on model M11:

    def cloth(n=6, z=0.45, integrator="implicitfast", solver="Newton", cone="pyramidal"):
    """Model M11: plane + box_stack(2) + an n x n cloth whose lowest point starts at z."""

    A plane, a stack of two 0.1 m boxes (model M4 with n=2, top face at z = 0.401) and a 6×6 cloth 0.05 above it, held together by an edge equality. No hfield and no SDF, which a flex model may not have. The task: choose integrator, solver, cone, iteration budget, tolerance, nconmax and njmax so that every world converges without any overflow bit, and among those settings take the one with the most world-steps per second.

    Because the cloth uses an edge equality and no elasticity, both IMPLICITFAST and DISCRETE are allowed: check_discrete only refuses flex elasticity under the implicit integrators. The script follows the Ch 9 recipe for the sizes and then checks the overflow bits, which matters here because of the flex candidate buffer (§5). Convergence is judged by OverflowType.ITERATIONS, which the solver sets for a world that reaches opt.iterations.

    What the run teaches:

    • Sizes first. The recipe (peak × 1.25) gave 48 contacts per world; the flex candidate buffer needed four times that. Every timing measured with an overflow bit set is meaningless, because dropped contacts change the physics.
    • Newton converges in 2-3 iterations, CG in 20-40. The 85 stiff equality rows make the problem badly conditioned for CG; Newton's factorization does not care. With iterations=50 every world converged with both, so no ITERATIONS bit; drop it to 20 and CG starts hitting the cap.
    • DISCRETE is not free. With no elasticity there is nothing for the effective metric to stabilize, so IMPLICITFAST is the faster valid choice here. Add <elasticity elastic2d="stretch"> to the cloth and IMPLICITFAST is refused; then the choice is DISCRETE or a much smaller Euler step.
    • Cone. Switching between pyramidal and elliptic changes the cost by tens of percent, in either direction depending on the solver; with only 4 worlds on a shared CPU the differences are close to the run-to-run noise, so measure on the GPU you will train on.

    On Colab

    The notebook runs the same sweep with NWORLD = 4096 and 1000 steps. Expect the per-iteration cost of Newton and CG to change relative to each other on a GPU: Newton's dense Hessian work and CG's extra iterations parallelize differently. Keep the selection rule (fastest config with no overflow bit) and let your numbers decide.

    Quiz: flexes and DISCRETE

    A flexcomp type="grid" count="6 6 1" dim="2" without pins. How many DOFs does it add?

    One body with 3 slide joints per vertex: 36 × 3 = 108, as Example 23.3 prints.

    You add <elasticity young="1e5" thickness="1e-3"/> to a dim=2 cloth and nothing changes. Why?

    For cloth, choose elastic2d="stretch", "bend" or "both"; with "none", m.flex_stiffness is all zeros (Example 23.2's pitfall).

    Which integrator settings can step a flex with stretch elasticity in 4.0.1?

    check_discrete refuses only implicit and implicitfast; its message says "set integrator='discrete', or 'Euler' with a small timestep", and RK4 passes too (forward.py::check_discrete).

    Which flex material requires DISCRETE, in both MuJoCo C and MJWarp?

    SNH's projected Hessian and the penalty contact terms only exist in the effective metric.

    A contact has geom = [3, -1], flex = [-1, 0], elem = [-1, 12], vert = [-1, -1]. What is it?

    Side 1 has geom = -1, so it is the flex side; its element id is 12 and no vertex is involved.

    Your cloth scene sets NARROWPHASE although nacon never exceeded half of naconmax. Most likely cause?

    _write_candidate prints "flex candidate overflow" and sets NARROWPHASE; raise nconmax (Example 23.9 needed 4x the recipe).

    What does derivative.eff_solve solve?

    Its docstring: "Solves the effective metric system M_hat * qacc = qfrc_eff", preconditioned by eff_prec (the backbone factor qHLD).

    Why does "discrete integrator: flex with general attachments requires solver='CG'" exist?

    Newton adds the CSR stiffness with _add_efmK_metric_dense; CG only needs products with M̂, which eff_mul_m provides for any attachment.

    13Summary

    Key takeaways

    • A flex is vertices (point masses on 3-slider bodies), edges and simplex elements; flexcomp builds them, smooth.flex computes flexvert_xpos, flexedge_length and flexedge_J per world.
    • Shape is kept by edge equalities (rows), elasticity (passive forces: stretch, bending, SNH, rods) or dim-1 edge springs. Cloth elasticity needs elastic2d; plasticity moves a rest state in d.plastic after each step.
    • Rods are discrete elastic rods: curvature binormals at hinges, twist on edges, frames by rod_parallel_transport. Trilinear flexes drive many vertices from 8 nodes per cell, corotationally.
    • Flex contacts are found after rigid ones, as candidates that are de-duplicated before they take pool slots. Classify a contact side as flex by geom == -1; size nconmax from the overflow bit.
    • DISCRETE solves (M + hD + h^2K)\,a = f - hKv: eff_build → eff_shift → eff_actuation + regularize_constraint → eff_solve (PCG) → the constraint solve in the same metric. It is stable for stiff flexes, dissipative, and costs more per step (about 3-4x Euler in the CPU run of Example 23.6; measure on your GPU).
    • 4.0.1 refuses hfield/SDF/rigid flexes, quadratic interpolation, self-collision and passive contact of interpolated flexes, flex edge equalities (equality="true") with sleep, SNH or passive contact without DISCRETE, elasticity under the implicit integrators, edge stiffness or noslip under DISCRETE, and Newton with general attachments.

    Stuck? See Troubleshooting (flex + hfield, overflow, NaN blow-ups) or the course map.
