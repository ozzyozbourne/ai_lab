One fixed-size page that stores records of different lengths and keeps them sorted. Slots (2-byte offsets, in key order) grow forward from the header; cells (the records) grow backward from the end of the page. Deleting leaves holes, holes are reused, and when the free bytes are not in one piece the page compacts.

The programs in this chapter use 4096-byte pages. Here the page is 256 bytes so that every byte is visible; the layout and the algorithm are the same, so every offset is 3840 lower (4096 − 256) than in the README and the solutions: banana@4080 there is banana@240 here. The self-test at the bottom replays the programs' scenario on 4096 bytes. Press Play, or step with → and ←. Then type your own keys.

What to look for

  • Insert in the middle. Only 2-byte slots move. The new cell is written wherever there is room.
  • Delete a cell in the middle. It becomes a free block: the hole stores its own size and the offset of the next hole in its first 4 bytes. Delete its neighbour and the two holes merge.
  • Insert something a little smaller than a hole. If fewer than 4 bytes are left over they cannot describe themselves: they become fragment bytes, known only as a count in the header.
  • Delete the lowest cell. No free block: the gap simply grows.
  • Scenario "Fragmented page". The page has enough free bytes for the new cell but no single piece is large enough, so the insert compacts first. Slot numbers stay; offsets change.
  • Reserved tail: 4 bytes. The content area ends 4 bytes before the end of the buffer, and no operation reads or writes those bytes: they belong to the caller. Chapter 22 keeps a page checksum there. The number is stored in the header byte reserved, so a page read back from disk knows where its content ends. The programs' scenario uses the default, 0.
  • Put, Delete key, Compact and Random op act on the page you are looking at. At the last step they are appended to the history. At an earlier step the history continues from there and the later steps are dropped (Reset reloads the scenario). Step back with ← or press Home to see how the page got into its shape.

The 16 header squares show values only. In order: cell_count (2 bytes), content_start (2), first_free (2, offset of the first free block, 0 = none), frag_bytes (1), kind (1, caller-defined), reserved (1), then the 7-byte user area. The line under the picture repeats them with names. Hover any byte for details.
