A write is in two places before it is safe: first in the operating system's cache (pending), and only after sync on the disk (durable). A power cut decides the fate of every pending write: dropped, kept, or torn, with only some of its eight 512-byte sectors written. Text and code: chapter README.

1. The chapter's program, step by step

This replays experiments 1, 2 and 4 of zig build run / cargo run on the same seeded simulated disk (seed 2121). A page is drawn as eight cells, one letter per sector: A is the old version, B the new one. Press Space to play, → to step, or tick Predict to be asked first.

2. Your turn: write, sync, crash

Choose a protocol, issue writes, and cut the power whenever you like. With double write, follow the order slot → sync → home → sync and try to lose a page; then skip the first sync and try again. Every action becomes a step, so you can scrub back through what happened. The same seed always tears the same way.

The JavaScript model behind this page is a port of the chapter's Zig and Rust code. It runs the whole demo program here and compares the text with expected_output.txt.
