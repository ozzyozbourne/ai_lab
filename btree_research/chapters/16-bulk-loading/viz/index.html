Building a B+tree from sorted keys, bottom-up. Leaves fill from left to right; each finished page hands one pair to the level above, and parents grow only when they are needed. Nothing is searched and nothing is split. Text and code: chapter 16 README.

1. The loader, one event at a time

Choose the input and the fill factor, then press Build and step through. The loader keeps one open page per level (dashed) and holds back the page before it as pending, so the last two pages of a level can still be evened out when the input ends. Tick Predict to be asked what happens next at the interesting moments.

2. The same keys, inserted one at a time

For comparison, this is the tree that ordinary put calls build from the keys chosen above. With ascending input every split leaves a half-full page behind that never fills up. "Page writes" counts every page dirtied by every operation, so it is an upper bound on physical writes: a buffer pool would absorb some of them, but not the reads, the splits or the extra pages.

3. Why not fill every page to 100%?

A tree of 10,000 keys with order 16 is bulk-loaded at three fill factors, then random keys are inserted. A packed tree is the smallest on the day it is built, and then nearly every insert finds a full leaf. At 1000 inserts the numbers are those of section 3 of the program output.
