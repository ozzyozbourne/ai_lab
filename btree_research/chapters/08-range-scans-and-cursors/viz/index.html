A range scan is one seek, then steps, then a stop test. This page shows the two ways a cursor can take those steps (along the leaf links, or along a remembered root-to-leaf path) and what a cursor has to do when the tree changes between two of its steps.

What to look for

  • One seek, one test. In the first figure the kind of the near bound only picks the seek ([ gives ≥, ( gives >); afterwards every key is tested against the far bound only. Try (6, 17] and [6, 17), then the same ranges backward.
  • Crossing a leaf boundary. The leaf-link cursor makes one move. The stack cursor goes up to the parent and down again, and from 12 to 15 in the canonical tree it has to pop a frame and go through the root: four moves. The counter node visits adds them up.
  • The stack is the position. With the stack design the path drawn into the tree (filled nodes, and a thick edge into the child each of them took) and the frame list in the cursor panel are the same thing, and they are the whole cursor. Nothing in the leaves points sideways for it.
  • A stale position. In the second figure run the scenario, then switch the mode to trust the old position and run it again: the same next returns 12 twice.
  • Deleting under the cursor. Seek to a key, delete that key, then press Next and Prev: the saved key still tells the cursor where to continue, although no entry has it any more.
