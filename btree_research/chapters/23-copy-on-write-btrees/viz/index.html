A copy-on-write B+tree never overwrites a page that a committed root can reach. A write copies the path from the root to the leaf, and a commit is one small write that makes the new root the current one. Old roots stay readable for as long as someone holds them, and the previous root always does: it is the fallback after a crash.

What to look for

  • Path copying. Step through a put. One page per level is copied, top down; the new root points at the copies and, for everything else, at the same pages as the old root. Tick Predict to be asked how many pages a put copies.
  • Two roots, shared subtrees. The picture always holds at least two roots once two commits exist: current and fallback (the root the other meta slot names). The pages under both are drawn once, with an edge from each root. A snapshot adds a third.
  • The root flip. Watch the meta page during a commit. Commits alternate between the two slots, so the slot of the newest commit is never the one being written. Tear the last commit shows why.
  • Pending, then free. The pages a commit replaces go to the pending list and keep their place in the store. They move to the free list one commit later, when the slot of the root that reached them has been overwritten, or later still if a snapshot is in the way. Every caption says which root holds which pending page.
  • Why one commit later. Crash a put + lose newest slot stops a transaction after its page writes and makes the newest meta slot unreadable. The restart lands on the older slot, and its tree is whole because the pages it needs were still pending.
  • The oldest kept root decides. Hold two snapshots, commit a few times, and release the newer one first: nothing is freed.
  • No leaf chain. A leaf split here creates a page that only its parent points at. The README explains why sibling links cannot survive path copying.
