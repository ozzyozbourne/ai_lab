The B+tree of chapter 07, with one change: a node is a 4096-byte page in a file, and a "pointer" is a page number. Put keys into it and watch pages fill up, split and get linked. Text of the chapter: README.md. Second page: the buffer pool during an insert that splits.

1. A tree of pages

Every box is one page of the file, labelled with its page id and the bytes in use. A page is full when its bytes run out, so a leaf with a few large values splits as early as a leaf with many small ones. Put next scenario key replays the 24 puts of the chapter's programs one at a time; or type your own key and value size.

Reading the figure. Page 0 is the metapage and is not drawn; page 1 is the root and stays the root. A line from an interior page starts between the two separators that bound the child it leads to; the dot is the parent's end of it (a page stores no pointer to its parent). In the scenario, key n carries a value of 300 + 24·n bytes. A new put always starts from the tree as it is after the put you are looking at has finished. Tick Predict to be asked, before each put is carried out, whether the cell fits.

An interior split. With 4096-byte pages an interior page holds hundreds of separators, so these 24 puts never fill one. Choose 128-byte toy pages (not a real format: 108 bytes for cells, values of at most 22 bytes) and press Run to the interior split: the first 25 puts of a 30-put scenario are applied at once, and put 26 is played step by step. Its leaf splits, the root has no room for the tenth separator, the root's content moves down for the second time, the interior page splits, and the tree has three levels with page 1 still on top.
