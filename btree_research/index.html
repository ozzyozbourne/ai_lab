The six studied codebases

The guide cites six real systems. Only two of them store their data in a B-tree; the other four are there for contrast and for techniques. The source reading map says which files to read.

  • PostgresB-tree: yes The main reference: an on-disk, concurrent B-link tree. Splits, split points, truncation, deduplication, vacuum, WAL.
  • TursoB-tree: yes The SQLite-format B-tree: slotted pages, balancing over siblings, page-stack cursors, pager, WAL frames.
  • Milvusin memory only Two small in-memory B-trees, a sorted-array index, LSM-like segments.
  • DuckDBB-tree: no Contrast: adaptive radix tree, zone maps, slab allocator with handles.
  • TigerBeetleB-tree: no Contrast: an LSM forest. Fuzzing style, static allocation, in-node search.
  • FAISSB-tree: no Contrast: IVF and HNSW show why ordered trees fail for vector similarity.

    Lower bound and upper bound derived from a loop invariant, the insertion-index result, ranges as two bound calls, the classic off-by-one traps, and galloping search.

  3. 03

    The definition as six invariants, the three meanings of "order", 2-3-4 trees and red-black trees, the height bound with its proof, get, in-order traversal and a first validate. Trees are built by hand.

    Read Search descent and invariants
  4. 04

    Insertion and splitting

    put into a leaf, the split and its promoted key, growth only at the root, what an insert costs, measured node fill, textbook top-down splitting, and reserve-then-change so that running out of memory cannot corrupt the tree.

    Read Interactive insert How full do the nodes get?
  5. 05

    Deletion and rebalancing

    The four repairs (predecessor, borrow, merge, root shrink), why an underflow travels up, and what real engines do instead. Its code is the complete in-memory B-tree of Part I.

  6. 06

    Invariants as code, a model-based fuzzer against an oracle, tiny nodes and swarm testing, asserted coverage counters, three deliberately broken trees, minimising a failing run, and fault injection.

  7. 07

    B+trees

    Values only in the leaves, copy-up versus push-up, the doubly linked leaf chain, duplicates through a tie-breaker, and nodes addressed by 32-bit handles into one array. Seven later chapters copy this code.

  8. 08

    Varints and records, comparator keys versus byte-comparable keys, and order-preserving encodings for integers, floats, strings, composite keys, NULLs and descending columns.
