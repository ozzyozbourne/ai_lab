HNSW keeps the other half of the tree idea: sparse upper levels for long steps, a dense bottom level for precision. It is a skip list whose "next key" has been replaced by "a neighbour that is closer", because points in a plane have no next.

1. Descent through the layers

The same 56 points appear on layer 0; about a third of them also live on layer 1, and a few of those on layer 2. On each layer a point is linked to its nearest points on that layer. Click any layer to place the query, then step. Try ef = 1 first and use the button to find a query it gets wrong.

What to take away

  • Coarse to fine survives. The top layer crosses the plane in one or two hops; lower layers refine.
  • Order does not. Without an order there is no "too far" test, so the search can stop at a point that merely has no closer neighbour. Keeping several candidates (ef) lets it walk around such a spot.
  • This picture is HNSW-like, not HNSW. Each point is simply linked to its nearest points per layer. The real construction inserts points one at a time and chooses links with a heuristic that keeps the graph better connected; FAISS stores all links in one flat array (see the chapter text).
