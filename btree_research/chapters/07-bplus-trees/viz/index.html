The same keys go into a classic B-tree and into a B+tree. Watch where an entry ends up when a node splits, what the leaf chain looks like, and which slot of the node array each B+tree node occupies.

What to look for

  • Copy-up and push-up. When a B+tree leaf splits, the first key of the new right leaf is copied into the parent and also stays in the leaf. When an internal node splits, the middle separator moves up and is in neither half, as in the classic B-tree. Tick Predict to be asked before each split.
  • Lookups. Search for 10 in the canonical trees: the B-tree stops in the root, the B+tree carries on to a leaf, because the 10 in its internal node is only a signpost.
  • Stale separators. Run the delete walk-through. After delete 15 the root of the B+tree still shows 15, dimmed: no leaf holds it any more, and the tree is still correct. Pause there and search for 15 or 16: the buttons always work on the trees you are looking at.
  • The leaf chain. The double-headed arrows between the leaves are the next and prev links. The line under the B+tree lists the same chain from first_leaf to last_leaf.
  • Handles. #n above a B+tree node is its index in the arena panel. Freed slots go to the head of the free list and are the first to be reused: delete until nodes are freed, then insert again.
  • Size. With the same tiny order the B+tree needs more nodes than the B-tree, because separators are extra copies. In a real page the opposite matters: internal nodes without values hold many more separators.
