    1MuJoCo basics

    Models, state, MJCF elements and options (Ch 1-2). 30 terms.

    act
    Actuator activation state, na numbers per world, for actuators with their own dynamics (integrator, filter, muscle, DC motor). Part of the physics state (State.ACT).Owner: Ch 12 §7 · also Ch 8 §4
    body
    A rigid frame with mass and inertia in the kinematic tree; it can carry geoms, sites, joints and child bodies.Owner: Ch 2 §2
    camera mode
    How a camera (or light) follows the model: fixed, track, trackcom, targetbody, targetbodycom (types.py::CamLightType); camlight applies it every forward.Owner: Ch 27 §7
    ctrl
    The control vector, one entry per control input (nu); actuators read it.Owner: Ch 2 §7
    default class
    A named set of default attribute values in MJCF that elements opt into with class=...Owner: Ch 2 §2
    DOF (degree of freedom)
    Degree of freedom: one independent direction of motion; nv counts them.Owner: Ch 2 §3 · also Ch 3 §4
    equality constraint
    A soft constraint that ties bodies, joints or tendons together: connect, weld, joint, tendon, flex.Owner: Ch 2 §5 · also Ch 8
    generalized coordinates
    The minimal set of numbers (qpos) that fixes the pose of every body, given the joints.Owner: Ch 2 §3 · also Ch 11 §1
    geom
    A geometric shape attached to a body, used for collision and drawing; it also contributes mass.Owner: Ch 2 §2 · also Ch 16 §1
    geom group
    An integer group (0-5) per geom (group=). Rays filter by it (the geomgroup argument of mjw.ray/rays) and the batch renderer by enabled_geom_groups (default 0, 1, 2).Owner: Ch 27 §2
    joint
    Declares how a body may move relative to its parent: free, ball, slide or hinge.Owner: Ch 2 §2
    joint limit
    A range on a hinge or slide joint (range="lo hi", limited). When the joint gets near an end it adds a one-sided constraint row; ball joints limit the rotation angle.Owner: Ch 19 §10 · also Ch 2 §3
    keyframe
    A named, stored state (qpos, qvel, act, ctrl, mocap, time) in the model.Owner: Ch 2 §9 · also Ch 8
    MJCF
    MuJoCo's XML model format: bodies, joints, geoms, actuators, sensors and options.Owner: Ch 2 §2 · also Ch 1 §2
    MjData
    The changing state of one simulation plus everything computed from it: qpos, qvel, contacts, forces.Owner: Ch 2 §1 · also Ch 1 §2, Ch 4, Ch 5
    MjModel
    Compiled, constant description of a MuJoCo scene: sizes, masses, geometry, options.Owner: Ch 2 §1 · also Ch 1 §2, Ch 4, Ch 5, Ch 7
    MjSpec
    MuJoCo's editable model specification: build or change a model as Python objects, then spec.compile() returns an MjModel. The course uses it for terrain and per-world mesh variants.Owner: Ch 2 §11 · also Ch 10 §12, Ch 18 §5
    mocap body
    A body with mocap=true: its pose is set by mocap_pos/mocap_quat, not by physics.Owner: Ch 2 §6 · also Ch 8
    MuJoCo
    Multi-Joint dynamics with Contact: a physics engine for robotics and biomechanics, maintained by Google DeepMind.Owner: Ch 1 §2
    qacc
    Generalized accelerations, one per DOF (nv). forward computes them (unconstrained qacc_smooth, then the solver's qacc); the integrator turns them into new qvel and qpos.Owner: Ch 2 §1 · also Ch 7 §1
    qfrc_* family
    Generalized-force arrays (nv per world), one per source: qfrc_spring, qfrc_damper, qfrc_gravcomp, qfrc_fluid (summed into qfrc_passive), plus qfrc_actuator, qfrc_applied, qfrc_bias, qfrc_constraint.Owner: Ch 12 §1 · also Ch 7 §5
    qpos
    Generalized positions: one number per position coordinate of every joint (a free body uses 7: x, y, z and a quaternion).Owner: Ch 1 §1
    quaternion
    Four numbers (w, x, y, z) with unit length that encode a 3-D rotation without singularities. MuJoCo and MJWarp store them scalar-first; wp.quat stores (x, y, z, w) (Ch 4 §6).Owner: Ch 2 §3 · also Ch 13 §2, Ch 3 §4
    qvel
    Generalized velocities: one number per degree of freedom (a free body uses 6: linear and angular velocity).Owner: Ch 1 §1
    set_const
    mjw.set_const(m, d): recompute the model constants derived from other fields (qpos0-dependent and fixed groups, SetConstGroup) on the device, for example after randomizing masses per world.Owner: Ch 10 §6 · also Ch 10 §7, Ch 29 §7
    site
    A massless marker frame on a body, used by sensors, tendons and actuators.Owner: Ch 2 §2
    solimp
    Five numbers (dmin, dmax, width, mid, power) that set a constraint's impedance d, between dmin at zero violation and dmax at width, as a sigmoid with its joint at mid.Owner: Ch 2 §4 · also Ch 19 §8
    solref
    Two numbers (timeconst, dampratio) that make a constraint behave like a spring-damper; negative values give stiffness and damping directly. The refsafe rule clamps timeconst to at least 2 timesteps.Owner: Ch 2 §4 · also Ch 10 §10, Ch 19 §8
    userdata
    d.userdata: a per-world float array of size nuserdata (MJCF <size nuserdata=...>) reserved for your own state; saved with State.USERDATA.Owner: Ch 26 §9 · also Ch 8 §4
    xfrc_applied
    User-applied Cartesian force and torque on each body, (nworld, nbody, 6) in the world frame at the body's centre of mass; mjw.xfrc_accumulate maps it to joint space.Owner: Ch 12 §11 · also Ch 7 §5

    2Physics primer

    Point masses, rigid bodies and time stepping from scratch (Ch 3). 14 terms.

    6-DOF state
    Position (3), orientation (unit quaternion, 4 numbers), linear velocity (3) and angular velocity (3) of a rigid body.Owner: Ch 3 §4
    angular velocity
    Vector omega: the body turns about omega/|omega| at |omega| rad/s.Owner: Ch 3 §4
    constraint
    A rule the state must (approximately) obey, enforced by a force the solver computes, e.g. no penetration, joint inside its range.Owner: Ch 3 §8
    explicit Euler
    Time stepping that updates position and velocity from the old state only: x += v dt, v += a dt.Owner: Ch 3 §2
    gyroscopic term
    omega x (I omega): the term in Euler's equation that couples the rotation axes of a spinning body.Owner: Ch 3 §5
    inertia tensor
    3x3 symmetric matrix I: angular momentum L = I omega. Its diagonal entries are moments of inertia.Owner: Ch 3 §5
    maximal coordinates
    Describe every body with its full 6-DOF pose, then add constraints that remove the DOFs a joint forbids.Owner: Ch 3 §7
    Newton's second law
    F = m a: the net force on a body equals its mass times its acceleration.Owner: Ch 3 §1
    penetration
    How far two shapes overlap. Soft contact models allow a small, controlled penetration.Owner: Ch 3 §3
    point mass
    An idealized body with mass but no size: it can move but not rotate.Owner: Ch 3 §1
    principal axes
    The eigenvectors of the inertia tensor: in this frame the tensor is diagonal.Owner: Ch 3 §5
    reduced (joint) coordinates
    Generalized (joint) coordinates: only the DOFs a joint allows, so the joint can never come apart.Owner: Ch 3 §7 · also Ch 11 §2
    rigid body
    A body whose points keep their distances: it can translate and rotate but not deform.Owner: Ch 3 §4
    semi-implicit (symplectic) Euler
    Symplectic Euler: update the velocity first, then move the position with the NEW velocity. MuJoCo's Euler integrator.Owner: Ch 3 §2 · also Ch 13 §3

    3Dynamics: equations of motion, forces, integrators

    What forward computes before the solver, and how a step advances (Ch 11-14, 23). 39 terms.

    actuator damping
    MuJoCo 4 linear (and polynomial) damping on an actuator's own coordinate (actuator_damping, actuator_dampingpoly), applied as a passive force.Owner: Ch 12 §2
    actuator dyn / gain / bias
    MuJoCo's actuator model: dyntype (DynType) says how act evolves; gaintype and biastype (GainType, BiasType) give force = gain · input + bias.Owner: Ch 12 §7 · also Ch 12 §8
    bias force
    c(q, qvel) = qfrc_bias: Coriolis, centrifugal and gravity forces in joint space, computed by RNE with qacc = 0.Owner: Ch 11 §1
    branch
    A root-to-leaf path in the body tree; MJWarp's kinematics runs one thread per (world, branch).Owner: Ch 11 §5
    cdof
    Per-DOF motion vector in the com frame: the spatial velocity produced by that DOF at unit speed; the columns of the body Jacobian.Owner: Ch 11 §7
    cinert
    Each body's inertia and mass expressed in the com frame (10 numbers per body), written by com_pos and summed up the tree by crb.Owner: Ch 11 §7
    com frame
    World-aligned frame centred at subtree_com of a tree's root body; all c-prefixed quantities (cdof, cinert, cvel, cacc, cfrc) use it.Owner: Ch 11 §3
    composite rigid body (crb)
    Composite rigid body: the summed com-frame inertia of a body and all its descendants (d.crb), used to build M.Owner: Ch 11 §8
    cvel
    Each body's spatial velocity (rotation, translation) in the com frame, written by com_vel in the velocity stage.Owner: Ch 11 §3 · also Ch 12 §4
    DC motor
    MuJoCo 4 actuator (DCMOTOR dyn, gain and bias types) that models an electric motor: voltage input, coil resistance and back-EMF.Owner: Ch 12 §8
    DISCRETE integrator
    MJWarp's integrator for stiff soft bodies: it solves the step (and the constraint problem) in an effective metric built from passive stiffness and damping (eff_build, eff_solve).Owner: Ch 23 §7 · also Ch 13 §7
    effective metric
    M_hat = M + h D + h^2 K: the mass matrix plus timestep-scaled damping and stiffness. DISCRETE solves the step (and the constraint problem) in this metric.Owner: Ch 23 §7
    equation of motion
    M(q) qacc + c(q, qvel) = tau + J^T f: Newton-Euler for a whole kinematic tree in joint coordinates.Owner: Ch 11 §1
    EULER integrator
    MuJoCo's default IntegratorType.EULER: semi-implicit Euler, with joint damping treated implicitly (unless DisableBit.EULERDAMP).Owner: Ch 13 §3
    fluid model
    MuJoCo's two fluid-force models: the inertia-box model (drag from each body's equivalent box, from density and viscosity) and the per-geom ellipsoid model (fluidshape="ellipsoid"). Result in qfrc_fluid.Owner: Ch 12 §3
    gravcomp
    Gravity compensation: a body's gravcomp attribute applies an upward force of gravcomp · m · g at its com, written to qfrc_gravcomp.Owner: Ch 12 §3
    gravity trick
    RNE adds gravity by giving the root an upward acceleration of -gravity instead of a weight force on every body; the result is the same bias force.Owner: Ch 12 §4 · also Ch 11 §4
    history buffer
    A per-world ring buffer of past (time, value) samples used for actuator and sensor delays and interval sampling.Owner: Ch 14 §8
    IMPLICITFAST integrator
    Implicit in velocity using the analytic derivative of the smooth forces (deriv_smooth_vel) but without the Coriolis/RNE term, so the matrix stays symmetric and can be Cholesky-factored.Owner: Ch 13 §5
    IMPLICIT integrator
    Implicit in velocity including the derivative of the RNE (Coriolis, centrifugal) forces; the matrix is not symmetric, so it is LU-factored.Owner: Ch 13 §6
    IMU
    Inertial measurement unit: an accelerometer and a gyro, often with a magnetometer.Owner: Ch 14 §3
    integrator
    The rule that advances qpos, qvel, act and time by one timestep from qacc (Euler, RK4, implicit, implicitfast, discrete).Owner: Ch 13 §1 · also Ch 7 §1
    inverse dynamics
    Computing the generalized force that produces a given acceleration at a given state.Owner: Ch 14 §9
    mass matrix
    The nv x nv symmetric positive definite joint-space inertia matrix M(q); 0.5 qvel^T M qvel is the kinetic energy.Owner: Ch 11 §1
    moment arm
    d(actuator_length)/d(qpos): how far a unit change of a joint moves the actuator; maps actuator force to joint torque.Owner: Ch 12 §6
    passive force
    A force that depends only on the state (q, qdot) and model parameters, not on ctrl: springs, dampers, gravity compensation, fluid drag.Owner: Ch 12 §2
    plasticity
    A spring whose reference moves when it is stretched past a yield distance, leaving a permanent deformation.Owner: Ch 12 §2
    polynomial stiffness and damping
    MuJoCo 4 accepts up to three numbers in stiffness and damping ("k p1 p2"): the first is linear, the other two go to jnt_stiffnesspoly / dof_dampingpoly as higher-order terms.Owner: Ch 12 §2
    quaternion integration
    Advancing an orientation by an angular velocity with the exponential map (math.py::quat_integrate), so the quaternion stays unit length, instead of adding q̇·dt.Owner: Ch 13 §2 · also Ch 3 §6
    RK4 (Runge-Kutta 4)
    Runge-Kutta 4: evaluates the dynamics four times per step and averages the slopes with weights 1/6, 1/3, 1/3, 1/6.Owner: Ch 13 §4
    RKMK (Runge-Kutta-Munthe-Kaas)
    Runge-Kutta-Munthe-Kaas: run Runge-Kutta on a rotation vector instead of the quaternion, and map stage velocities with the derivative of the exponential map.Owner: Ch 13 §4
    RNE (recursive Newton-Euler)
    Recursive Newton-Euler: an inverse-dynamics algorithm with a forward pass over bodies (accelerations) and a backward pass (forces).Owner: Ch 12 §4
    sensor
    A measurement declared in the MJCF <sensor> block and computed by forward into d.sensordata.Owner: Ch 14 §1
    sparse actuator moment
    d.actuator_moment stored row-sparse: per actuator only its nonzero DOF entries, indexed by moment_rownnz, moment_rowadr, moment_colind.Owner: Ch 12 §6
    spatial vector
    A 6-number vector (rotation part, translation part): a twist (angular, linear velocity) or a wrench (torque, force).Owner: Ch 11 §3
    specific force
    Acceleration minus gravitational acceleration: what an accelerometer measures.Owner: Ch 14 §3
    tendon
    A scalar length defined from joints (fixed tendon) or from a path through sites and wrapping geoms (spatial tendon), with its own Jacobian.Owner: Ch 12 §10
    transmission
    The mapping from joint coordinates to an actuator's own length coordinate; its derivative is the moment arm.Owner: Ch 12 §6
    wrap (tendon wrapping)
    A path element of a spatial tendon: the tendon passes through sites and wraps around sphere or cylinder geoms, and its length and Jacobian follow the wrapped path.Owner: Ch 12 §10

    4Math toolkit

    Linear algebra and optimization the solvers rely on (Ch 11, 19-20). 15 terms.

    Cholesky factorization
    Factorization A = UᵀU (or LLᵀ) of an SPD matrix into triangular factors, used to solve A x = b cheaply.Owner: Ch 20 §6
    complementarity
    A pair of non-negative quantities whose product must be zero: one of them is always zero.Owner: Ch 19 §1
    conjugate gradient
    An iterative method that combines the current steepest-descent direction with the previous search direction so successive steps do not undo each other.Owner: Ch 20 §5
    convex function
    A function whose graph lies below every chord; any local minimum is the global minimum.Owner: Ch 19 §2
    CSR
    Compressed sparse row: values row by row with a start address and a count per row; d.M uses it for the lower triangle of M.Owner: Ch 19 §7 · also Ch 11 §8
    factorization
    Writing the SPD mass matrix M as L D L^T (stored in qLD, qLDiagInv) so that solves with M are cheap.Owner: Ch 7 §3
    gradient descent
    Repeatedly stepping along the negative gradient. Simple, but slow when the problem is badly conditioned; CG and Newton fix that.Owner: Ch 19 §2
    Hager-Zhang
    A conjugate-gradient beta formula with stronger convergence guarantees than Polak-Ribière; the dev tree uses it with a non-negative clamp.Owner: Ch 20 §5
    LDL factorization
    Writing an SPD matrix as L D LT with unit-triangular L and diagonal D; MJWarp stores the mass-matrix factor in qLD and qLDiagInv (factor_m).Owner: Ch 11 §9
    linesearch
    The 1-D search for a good step length along the solver's search direction, run inside every solver iteration.Owner: Ch 20 §8 · also Ch 5 §9, Ch 19 §2
    Newton's method
    An optimization method that steps along -H^-1 grad, the minimizer of the local quadratic model.Owner: Ch 20 §6
    piecewise quadratic
    A function made of quadratic pieces joined continuously; its curvature jumps at the joints (kinks).Owner: Ch 19 §2 · also Ch 20 §1
    preconditioner
    A cheap approximate inverse applied to the gradient so the problem looks better conditioned to the solver.Owner: Ch 20 §5 · also Ch 23 §7
    quadratic form
    A function f(x) = 0.5 x^T A x - b^T x: the multi-dimensional parabola.Owner: Ch 19 §2
    SPD
    Symmetric positive definite: symmetric with all eigenvalues > 0; x^T A x > 0 for every x != 0.Owner: Ch 19 §2 · also Ch 20 §6

    5Warp and the GPU

    Kernels, threads, memory, compilation and CUDA graphs (Ch 4, 7, 24, 26). 36 terms.

    atomics (atomic operations)
    Read-modify-write operations (wp.atomic_add, ...) that no other thread can interrupt.Owner: Ch 4 §9
    block (thread block)
    A group of threads (block_dim, default 256 in Warp) that runs on one SM and can share memory.Owner: Ch 4 §4 · also Ch 25 §2
    coalescing (coalesced access)
    Neighbouring threads of a warp reading neighbouring addresses, so the hardware serves the warp with few memory transactions.Owner: Ch 4 §4
    conditional node
    A CUDA graph node that repeats (capture_while) or skips (capture_if) a sub-graph based on a value in device memory.Owner: Ch 24 §5 · also Ch 6 §10, Ch 7 §10, Ch 20 §11
    CUDA graph
    A recording of a sequence of GPU launches that can be replayed with one call (wp.ScopedCapture, wp.capture_launch).Owner: Ch 24 §1 · also Ch 7 §10, Ch 10
    CUDA stream
    A queue of GPU work; work in one stream runs in order, different streams may overlap.Owner: Ch 28 §2
    debug mode
    wp.config.mode = "debug", set before the first launch: Warp compiles bounds checks into every array access. Slow, but it turns silent out-of-bounds writes into errors.Owner: Ch 26 §6 · also Ch 9 §2
    device
    Where Warp arrays live and kernels run: "cpu" or a GPU such as "cuda:0".Owner: Ch 4 §1
    divergence
    Lanes of one warp taking different branches; the warp executes each taken branch in turn with the other lanes masked off.Owner: Ch 4 §4
    event
    A timestamp marker placed in a GPU stream; the time between two events is measured on the device.Owner: Ch 7 §9
    graph_conditional
    m.opt.graph_conditional: use conditional graph nodes for device-side loops. It needs CUDA 12.4+ toolkit and driver; put_model sets it True, so on older CUDA set it False before capturing.Owner: Ch 24 §6
    grid
    All blocks of one kernel launch.Owner: Ch 4 §4
    JIT compilation
    Just-in-time compilation: code is compiled the first time it is needed, at run time, not ahead of time.Owner: Ch 4 §8 · also Ch 1 §4, Ch 5
    kernel
    A function that runs on the device once per thread index of a launch.Owner: Ch 4 §3 · also Ch 1 §4, Ch 5, Ch 7 §8, Ch 24 §1, Ch 26
    kernel cache
    The directory where Warp stores compiled kernel binaries so later runs can load them instead of compiling again.Owner: Ch 4 §8 · also Ch 1 §5
    launch dim
    The dim argument of wp.launch: how many threads, and in what shape, run the kernel.Owner: Ch 7 §8
    launch-shape atlas
    The course's catalogue of the eight ways MJWarp maps work to threads (Ch 7 owns layouts 1, 2, 6, 7, 8).Owner: Ch 7 §8 · also Ch 27 §8
    log level
    wp.config.log_level: how much Warp prints. Course scripts set wp.LOG_WARNING right after import warp to silence module-load messages.Owner: Ch 1 §5
    occupancy
    Resident warps on an SM divided by the maximum it can hold; higher occupancy hides memory latency better.Owner: Ch 4 §4 · also Ch 6 §11, Ch 27 §9
    PCIe
    The bus between the CPU and a discrete GPU; every host-device copy crosses it.Owner: Ch 5 §6
    shared memory
    Fast on-chip memory of an SM, shared by the threads of one block.Owner: Ch 4 §4
    SIMT
    Single instruction, multiple threads: the 32 lanes of a warp execute the same instruction, each on its own data.Owner: Ch 4 §4
    SM (streaming multiprocessor)
    Streaming Multiprocessor: a GPU core cluster that runs warps, with its own registers and shared memory.Owner: Ch 4 §4 · also Ch 6 §11
    tile
    A block-wide array that the threads of one block load, compute on and store together (shared memory on GPU).Owner: Ch 26 §8 · also Ch 4 §11, Ch 27 §1
    vec3
    Warp's 3-float vector dtype (12 bytes); one array element holds a whole vector.Owner: Ch 6 §2
    vec3 / quat / mat33 / spatial_vector
    Warp's fixed-size value types: one array element holds a whole 3-vector, quaternion (stored x, y, z, w), 3x3 matrix or 6-vector, and NumPy views add a trailing dimension.Owner: Ch 6 §2 · also Ch 4 §6
    warp (32 lanes)
    32 GPU threads that execute one instruction stream in lockstep (SIMT).Owner: Ch 4 §4
    Warp (NVIDIA Warp)
    NVIDIA's Python framework for writing GPU (and CPU) kernels in Python, JIT-compiled to CUDA or C++.Owner: Ch 1 §4 · also Ch 4 §1
    wp.array
    Warp's typed, device-resident n-dimensional array (up to 4 dims).Owner: Ch 4 §2
    wp.capture_if
    A conditional graph node: runs one of two captured branches depending on an int in device memory.Owner: Ch 24 §5
    wp.capture_launch
    wp.capture_launch(graph): replay a captured CUDA graph with one call.Owner: Ch 24 §2
    wp.capture_while
    A conditional graph node: repeats a captured body while an int in device memory is nonzero. MJWarp's solver loop uses it when m.opt.graph_conditional is True.Owner: Ch 24 §5 · also Ch 20 §2
    wp.func (@wp.func)
    A Warp device function: typed, callable from kernels and other funcs, may return values; inlined by the compiler.Owner: Ch 4 §5
    wp.launch_tiled
    wp.launch_tiled(kernel, dim, inputs, block_dim): launch block_dim threads per logical index so they can load and compute tiles together.Owner: Ch 26 §8 · also Ch 4 §11
    wp.ScopedCapture
    with wp.ScopedCapture() as cap: records the launches made inside the block into cap.graph instead of running them.Owner: Ch 24 §2 · also Ch 7 §10
    wp.tid()
    Inside a kernel, returns this thread's launch index (one int per launch dimension).Owner: Ch 4 §3

    6Batching, sizing and driving simulations

    Many worlds in one Data, buffer sizes and overflow (Ch 5-10). 23 terms.

    batched field
    A Model array whose type spec starts with '*': its leading dimension may be 1 (shared) or B (one row per group of worlds).Owner: Ch 6 §1 · also Ch 10
    contact pool
    One flat array of naconmax contact slots shared by all worlds; each contact records its worldid.Owner: Ch 9 §2 · also Ch 6 §8
    domain randomization
    Training or testing on many simulator variants whose physical or visual parameters are drawn from distributions, so the result is robust to the real values.Owner: Ch 10 §1
    forward dynamics (mjw.forward)
    mjw.forward: computes every derived quantity (positions, contacts, forces, accelerations) from the current state without advancing time.Owner: Ch 7 §1
    get_data_into
    mjw.get_data_into(mjd, mjm, d, world_id=0): copy one world back into an existing host MjData. Every call crosses PCIe, so call it rarely.Owner: Ch 5 §6
    make_data
    mjw.make_data(mjm, nworld, ...): allocate a fresh device Data for nworld worlds in the default state, with the buffer sizes (nconmax, njmax, ...) you pass.Owner: Ch 5 §4
    mjw.State (PHYSICS, INTEGRATION, WARMSTART)
    Integer bit flags naming state components (TIME, QPOS, QVEL, ACT, HISTORY, WARMSTART, CTRL, ...). PHYSICS = QPOS | QVEL | ACT | HISTORY | PLASTIC; INTEGRATION sets every bit (all inputs of a step); add WARMSTART to PHYSICS for bitwise replays.Owner: Ch 8 §4 · also Ch 22 §7
    nacon
    d.nacon: a one-element device counter of contacts written to the shared pool this step, over all worlds.Owner: Ch 9 §2
    naconmax
    Total contact slots in the shared pool, for all worlds together; make_data sets it to nconmax × nworld by default.Owner: Ch 6 §1
    nccdmax / naccdmax
    Scratch rows for convex (GJK/EPA) collision: nccdmax per world, naccdmax for all worlds (defaults to naconmax). Too few sets the CCD overflow bit.Owner: Ch 9 §5 · also Ch 17 §7
    ncollision
    d.ncollision: the number of candidate pairs the broadphase wrote (one counter for all worlds).Owner: Ch 15 §2
    nconmax
    make_data/put_data argument: contacts to allocate per world. The pool holds naconmax = nconmax × nworld slots, and one world may use more than nconmax.Owner: Ch 9 §5
    njmax
    Maximum constraint (efc) rows per world; each world has its own njmax rows.Owner: Ch 6 §1
    njmax_nnz
    Non-zeros of the sparse constraint Jacobian per world; defaults to njmax × nv for dense models; for sparse models make_data estimates it from the model’s frictionloss, limit and contact rows, at most njmax × nv.Owner: Ch 9 §5 · also Ch 19 §7
    nworld
    The number of worlds in one mjw.Data. Every per-world state array (qpos, qvel, ctrl, time, ...) has nworld as its first dimension; contacts are pooled across worlds instead (Ch 6, Ch 9).Owner: Ch 6 §1 · also Ch 1 §3
    overflow bitmask
    d.overflow, one int per world: sticky OverflowType bits (NEFC, NJMAX_NNZ, BROADPHASE, NARROWPHASE, CCD, HFIELD, ...) set when a buffer was too small and work was dropped.Owner: Ch 9 §7
    pipeline breadcrumb
    The fixed strip of pipeline stations at the top of Ch 7-23; each chapter highlights the stations it explains.Owner: Ch 7 §2
    put_data
    mjw.put_data(mjm, mjd, nworld, ...): copy one host MjData to the device, repeated in every world.Owner: Ch 5 §4
    put_model
    mjw.put_model(mjm): copy a compiled MjModel to the device as an mjw.Model; batch_sizes gives chosen '*' fields B rows.Owner: Ch 6 §6
    round-robin batching
    A batched Model field with B rows is applied to world w as row w % B: rows are dealt to worlds like cards (worlds k, k+B, k+2B, ... share row k), not in contiguous blocks.Owner: Ch 6 §7 · also Ch 10 §2
    state signature
    An integer bitmask of mjw.State flags that says which state components are packed into a state vector.Owner: Ch 8 §4
    sticky bit
    A flag that stays set once set, until explicitly cleared (here by reset_data).Owner: Ch 9 §7
    world
    One independent simulation inside a batched MJWarp Data; worlds share one model but have their own state.Owner: Ch 6 §1 · also Ch 1 §3, Ch 5 §1

    7Collision

    Broadphase, narrowphase, convex and SDF collision, flex geometry (Ch 15-18, 23). 40 terms.

    AABB
    Axis-aligned bounding box: a box aligned with the world axes that contains a shape.Owner: Ch 15 §1 · also Ch 27
    bounding sphere
    The sphere of radius geom_rbound around a geom's centre; the cheapest broadphase filter (BroadphaseFilter.SPHERE).Owner: Ch 15 §4 · also Ch 15 §1
    broadphase
    A cheap test that lists geom pairs that might touch (bounding volumes overlap).Owner: Ch 15 §2 · also Ch 9 §1
    CCD (convex collision detection)
    The general path for convex pairs with no primitive formula: GJK for distance, EPA for penetration, then multi-contact (collision_gjk.py, collision_convex.py).Owner: Ch 17 §1
    collision table
    The list of geom-type pairs and which narrowphase (primitive, convex/CCD, SDF, heightfield) handles each; only pairs present in the model are compiled.Owner: Ch 16 §1
    condim
    Contact dimension: 1 = normal force only, 3 = normal + 2 tangential friction directions, 4 and 6 add torsional and rolling friction.Owner: Ch 9 §4 · also Ch 5 §6, Ch 16 §6
    contact
    A point where two geoms touch or nearly touch, with a distance, a position and a frame.Owner: Ch 9 §1
    ContactType
    Bits on each contact (types.py::ContactType): CONSTRAINT = 1 (for the solver), SENSOR = 2 (for a collision sensor), PASSIVE = 4 (for the discrete integrator's effective metric).Owner: Ch 9 §11
    contype / conaffinity
    Per-geom bitmasks: two geoms may collide when (contype1 & conaffinity2) || (contype2 & conaffinity1). put_model uses them to pre-filter pairs.Owner: Ch 15 §3
    elliptic cone
    The exact friction cone (ConeType.ELLIPTIC): one row per contact dimension, with the cone constraint handled by the solver's CONE state.Owner: Ch 9 §4 · also Ch 19 §9
    EPA
    Expanding Polytope Algorithm: grows a polytope inside the Minkowski difference until its face closest to the origin gives the penetration depth and normal.Owner: Ch 17 §1
    flex
    A deformable object in MuJoCo: vertices (point masses on bodies) joined by edges and simplex elements; dim 1 = rope, 2 = cloth, 3 = soft solid.Owner: Ch 23 §1
    flexcomp
    MJCF macro that generates a flex plus one body (with 3 slide joints) per vertex: types grid, box, cylinder, ellipsoid, mesh, gmsh, direct.Owner: Ch 23 §1
    friction cone
    The set of allowed contact forces; MuJoCo approximates it as a pyramid (default) or uses the exact elliptic cone.Owner: Ch 9 §4 · also Ch 5 §6
    gap
    Extra distance beyond the margin in which contacts are detected but not active (unless adhesive).Owner: Ch 16 §5
    GeomType
    Enum of geom shapes: PLANE, HFIELD, SPHERE, CAPSULE, ELLIPSOID, CYLINDER, BOX, MESH, SDF (plus internal FLEX, TRIANGLE). The pair of types picks the collider.Owner: Ch 16 §1
    GJK
    Gilbert-Johnson-Keerthi: finds the distance between two convex shapes, or proves they overlap, using only support points of their Minkowski difference.Owner: Ch 17 §1
    grid-stride loop
    A kernel pattern where T threads cover N items: thread t handles items t, t+T, t+2T, ... so the launch size does not have to equal the work size.Owner: Ch 17 §7
    Halton sequence
    A low-discrepancy sequence: the radical inverse of the index in a prime base. Fills space more evenly than random points.Owner: Ch 18 §7
    heightfield
    A geom whose surface is a regular grid of elevation samples, used for terrain.Owner: Ch 18 §1
    heightfield prism
    One grid cell of a heightfield split into triangular prisms; the convex collider tests a geom against the prisms under its bounding box.Owner: Ch 18 §2
    horizon
    In EPA: the loop of edges between the faces a new support point can see (deleted) and the faces it cannot (kept). New faces connect the point to every horizon edge.Owner: Ch 17 §5
    margin
    Distance below which a contact becomes an active constraint (includemargin).Owner: Ch 16 §5
    Minkowski difference
    A - B = {a - b : a in A, b in B}. It contains the origin exactly when A and B overlap; its distance to the origin is the distance between A and B.Owner: Ch 17 §2
    multiccd
    Generating several contacts for one convex pair (face clipping around the EPA witness), so flat contacts are stable; DisableBit.MULTICCD turns it off.Owner: Ch 17 §6
    narrowphase
    The exact geometric test for one candidate pair; it writes 0 or more contacts.Owner: Ch 16 §4 · also Ch 9 §1, Ch 15 §1
    NXN
    The brute-force broadphase: test every pair of the filtered list in every world.Owner: Ch 15 §5
    OBB
    Oriented bounding box: a box aligned with the shape's own frame.Owner: Ch 15 §1
    octree SDF
    An SDF sampled on the corners of an adaptive octree and interpolated trilinearly.Owner: Ch 18 §6
    primitive collider
    A hand-derived formula for one pair of simple shapes (plane-sphere, capsule-capsule, ...) that returns distance, position and normal directly.Owner: Ch 16 §1
    pyramidal cone
    The default friction-cone approximation (ConeType.PYRAMIDAL): condim 1 gives one normal row; condim c > 1 gives 2(c-1) rows, each a non-negative force along one edge of a pyramid.Owner: Ch 9 §4 · also Ch 19 §9
    rod
    A 1-D flex with bending and twist elasticity, simulated as a discrete elastic rod (Bergou et al. 2008): curvature at hinges, twist per edge.Owner: Ch 23 §3
    SAP (sweep and prune)
    Sweep and prune: project bounding volumes onto an axis, sort them, and only test pairs whose intervals overlap.Owner: Ch 15 §6
    SDF (signed distance field)
    Signed distance field: a function giving the distance to a surface, negative inside, positive outside.Owner: Ch 18 §6
    separating axis theorem
    Two convex shapes are disjoint exactly when some direction exists on which their projections do not overlap.Owner: Ch 15 §1 · also Ch 16 §8
    simplex
    The simplest shape in a dimension: a point, a segment, a triangle or a tetrahedron (1 to 4 points in 3-D).Owner: Ch 17 §3
    support function
    s(d): the point of a shape that lies furthest along direction d. A convex shape is fully described by it.Owner: Ch 17 §1
    surface velocity
    A geom attribute (6 numbers, linear then angular, local frame) that makes contacts behave as if the surface moved, without moving the geom.Owner: Ch 16 §9
    user SDF plugin
    A geom SDF evaluated by a user-supplied Warp function (user_sdf / user_sdf_grad) selected by the plugin type id.Owner: Ch 18 §8
    witness points
    The pair of points, one on each shape, that realize the distance (or the penetration) between them. The contact position is their midpoint.Owner: Ch 17 §3

    8Constraints, solver, islands and determinism

    Constraint rows, the primal solvers, sleeping and reproducibility (Ch 19-22). 27 terms.

    aref
    Per-row reference acceleration, aref = -b · (J v) - k · d · pos: the spring-damper target the soft constraint pulls toward.Owner: Ch 19 §8
    bitwise reproducible
    Two runs produce exactly the same bit patterns in every compared array, not just values that are close.Owner: Ch 22 §1
    CG solver
    SolverType.CG: nonlinear conjugate gradient on the primal problem, M-preconditioned (Polak-Ribiere in 4.0.1), cheap per iteration.Owner: Ch 20 §5
    chaos (chaotic dynamics)
    Sensitive dependence on initial conditions: tiny differences grow exponentially until trajectories are unrelated.Owner: Ch 22 §5
    constraint Jacobian (efc J)
    The constraint Jacobian: one row per scalar constraint, mapping generalized velocity (nv) to the constraint's velocity.Owner: Ch 19 §7
    constraint row (efc row)
    One scalar constraint of the solver (efc = equality, friction, contact); MuJoCo stores them as rows of a matrix.Owner: Ch 19 §4 · also Ch 5 §1, Ch 7 §6, Ch 9 §1
    ConstraintState
    Per-row label (SATISFIED, QUADRATIC, LINEARNEG, LINEARPOS, CONE) saying which piece of its cost a row is on at the current qacc.Owner: Ch 20 §1
    determinism (CONTACTS / CONSTRAINT / ATOMICS)
    m.opt.deterministic, a DeterminismType bitmask. In 4.0.1 only ATOMICS does something (run-to-run invariant atomic reductions); CONTACTS and CONSTRAINT sorting are stubs there.Owner: Ch 22 §3 · also Ch 22 §8
    DSU (disjoint-set union)
    Disjoint-set union (union-find): a forest of parent pointers where each set is identified by its root; union links two roots, find walks to the root.Owner: Ch 21 §2
    efc.D
    Per-row weight of the constraint cost, D = 1/R with R the row's regularizer from impedance: soft rows have small D.Owner: Ch 19 §8
    frictionloss
    Dry friction on a joint or tendon: a friction row whose force is bounded by the frictionloss value (rows of type FRICTION_DOF / FRICTION_TENDON).Owner: Ch 19 §4
    Gauss principle
    The constrained acceleration minimizes the M-weighted distance to the unconstrained one plus the constraint cost; MuJoCo's soft version is a convex problem in qacc.Owner: Ch 19 §3
    impedance
    d in (0, 1) from solimp: how much of the constraint is enforced at the current violation; it sets D and scales aref.Owner: Ch 19 §8
    impratio
    m.opt.impratio: ratio of frictional to normal impedance for elliptic cones; larger values make friction stiffer (less slip).Owner: Ch 19 §9
    island
    A maximal group of kinematic trees connected through constraint rows. Trees in different islands do not influence each other within a step.Owner: Ch 21 §1
    kinematic tree
    A body hanging directly off the world body, together with all its descendants. Trees are the unit of sleeping.Owner: Ch 21 §1
    Newton solver
    SolverType.NEWTON (default): uses the Hessian H = M + JT D J and its Cholesky factor; few iterations, more work each.Owner: Ch 20 §6
    noslip
    A post-processing pass (projected Gauss-Seidel over friction rows with the unregularized Delassus operator) that removes the slip of soft friction.Owner: Ch 20 §13
    nvmax
    make_data/put_data argument: the maximum number of awake DOFs per world the compacted sleep solve can hold. Defaults to nv.Owner: Ch 21 §8
    PGS (projected Gauss-Seidel)
    Projected Gauss-Seidel: sweep over the rows, solve each one exactly with the others fixed, and project the result back onto its feasible set.Owner: Ch 20 §13
    primal solver
    A solver that searches directly over the accelerations qacc (the primal variables) instead of over the constraint forces.Owner: Ch 20 §1
    refsafe
    The rule timeconst ← max(timeconst, 2 · timestep), applied unless DisableBit.REFSAFE is set, so a too-stiff solref cannot blow up a step.Owner: Ch 19 §8
    replay testing
    Checking reproducibility by recording a run (states, ctrl) and replaying it from a snapshot, comparing bit patterns or short horizons rather than long chaotic trajectories.Owner: Ch 22 §6
    sleep policy
    Per-tree tree_sleep_policy chosen by the compiler (AUTO_NEVER for actuated trees, AUTO_ALLOWED otherwise); MJWarp 4.0.1 rejects the user policies NEVER, ALLOWED and INIT.Owner: Ch 21 §4 · also Ch 21 §7
    sleep (sleeping trees)
    Freezing a kinematic tree (zero qvel and qacc, no contacts with static geometry or other sleepers) after it has been still for mjMINAWAKE steps, until something wakes it.Owner: Ch 21 §3
    ulp
    Unit in the last place: the gap between a float and the next representable float. For float32 near 1.0 it is about 1.2e-7.Owner: Ch 22 §5
    warmstart
    The constraint solver's initial guess for qacc, taken from the previous step (d.qacc_warmstart).Owner: Ch 20 §12 · also Ch 8 §4
