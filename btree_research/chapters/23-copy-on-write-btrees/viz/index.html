A copy-on-write B+tree never overwrites a page that a committed root can reach. A write copies the path from the root to the leaf, and a commit is one small write that makes the new root the current one. Old roots stay readable for as long as someone holds them.

What to look for

  • Path copying. Step through a put. One page per level is copied, top down; the new root points at the copies and, for everything else, at the same pages as the old root. Tick Predict to be asked how many pages a put copies.
  • Two roots, shared subtrees. Take a snapshot, then put a key. Both roots stay on screen: the pages under both are drawn once, with an edge from each root.
  • The root flip. Watch the meta page on the right during a commit. Commits alternate between the two slots, so the slot of the newest commit is never the one being written. Tear the last commit shows why.
  • Pending, then free. Pages replaced while a snapshot is held go to the pending list and keep their place in the store. Release the snapshot and they move to the free list; the next put reuses their page ids instead of growing the store.
  • The oldest reader decides. Hold two snapshots, commit a few times, and release the newer one first: nothing is freed.
  • No leaf chain. A leaf split here creates a page that only its parent points at. The README explains why sibling links cannot survive path copying.
