The same workload under every policy. This page runs the chapter's model in your browser (the same code path as the Zig and Rust programs, checked against their output below) and shows what each policy costs in pages and in churn. Back to the step-by-step figure or the chapter text.

How to read it

  • Leaf fill profile: one thin column per leaf page, left to right in key order. The height is how full the page is. A solid block means full pages; a comb at half height means half-empty pages.
  • leaf writes counts pages rewritten by structural work only (split, balance, merge), not the one write every insert or delete needs anyway.
  • onto old pages counts cells that were moved onto a page that already existed. A plain split and the append fast path keep it at zero. Postgres needs it to be zero, because its scans wait between pages; that is why it never redistributes or merges.
  • Press play to watch the workload progress, or drag the scrubber.

Cells are 40 to 200 bytes, pages 4096 bytes. With 20,000 keys the numbers are exactly those in expected_output.txt; the badge at the bottom of the page confirms it. Counts from this model on these workloads; not a benchmark of any engine.
