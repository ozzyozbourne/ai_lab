    Part IV · Chapter 17

    Collision III: Convex Shapes with GJK, EPA and Multi-Contact

    Spheres, planes and capsules have closed-form colliders. Everything else that is convex (cylinders, ellipsoids, boxes, meshes) goes through one general algorithm: ask each shape "which of your points lies furthest in this direction?", and let GJK, EPA and a polygon clipper turn those answers into contacts. This chapter takes that path apart, from the support function to the grid-stride kernel and its scratch memory.

    Expert About 120 min· Prerequisites: Ch 16 (and through it Ch 15, Ch 9)

    You will learn

    • Follow GJK in 2-D and 3-D: the signed-volume sub-algorithm _S1D/_S2D/_S3D, the duality-gap stop, the separation cutoff, and the shrink-and-inflate trick of gjk_phase.
    • Follow EPA: the start polytopes _polytope2/3/4, the closest face, the horizon (_add_edge), face bit packing, MJ_MAX_EPAHORIZON and the witness points.
    • Explain multi-contact (multicontact, _polygon_clip, _polygon_quad) and when it runs.
    • Size naccdmax, tune ccd_iterations / ccd_tolerance, and read the CCD and EPA_HORIZON overflow bits.
    • Predict how a non-convex mesh collides: as its convex hull, found by hill-climbing.

    Before you start: recap

    • Collision runs in two stages: the broadphase writes candidate geom pairs, the narrowphase turns each candidate into 0 or more contacts (Ch 15).
    • MJ_COLLISION_TABLE sends each geom-type pair to PRIMITIVE (closed form) or CONVEX (this chapter); box-box is CONVEX unless DisableBit.NATIVECCD is set (Ch 16).
    • Every collider ends in collision_core.py::write_contact: dist < margin + gap is kept, a slot is taken with wp.atomic_add, frame[0] points from geom[0] to geom[1] (Ch 16).
    • All worlds share one contact pool of naconmax slots; overflow sets a bit in d.overflow and drops contacts silently (Ch 9).
    • 32 lanes run in lockstep; branches serialize; contiguous reads coalesce; Ch 4 §4.

    1Why one general convex path

    Ch 16 showed colliders that solve one pair of shapes in closed form: plane-sphere is one dot product, capsule-capsule is the closest points of two segments. That approach does not scale. With 8 rigid geom types there are 36 type pairs, and pairs such as cylinder-ellipsoid or mesh-mesh have no neat formula at all. MJWarp therefore sends the hard pairs to a single algorithm that only needs one thing from each shape: its support function.

    The path has three stages, all in mujoco_warp/_src/collision_gjk.py:

    1. GJK finds the distance between two convex shapes, or proves that they overlap.
    2. EPA runs only when they overlap and recovers the penetration depth and normal.
    3. Multi-contact turns the single answer into up to 4 contacts when two flat faces touch, so a box can rest without rocking.

    There is no MPR (Minkowski Portal Refinement, the other classic convex algorithm) in this code base: collision_gjk.py has GJK, EPA and polygon clipping only. Figure 1 shows which pairs take this path and where each stage lives.

    CAPSULE, ELLIPSOID / CYLINDER / MESHCONVEXsegment vs curved or faceted surface
    ELLIPSOID, * (except plane) · CYLINDER, CYLINDER / BOX / MESHCONVEXcurved surfaces on both sides

    Example 17.1 builds one scene with three of these pairs and shows that the convex path produces the contacts you would expect: 4 for the flat cylinder and mesh faces, 1 for the round ellipsoid.

    Two details to notice. The pair order follows the type order (cylinder = 5 < box = 6, but box = 6 < mesh = 7), so the mesh contact is reported as table_mesh-hex: frame[0] points from the box into the mesh. And the CCD contacts carry the same dist convention as the primitive ones (the 1 mm we placed them into each other), even though GJK worked on margin-inflated shapes internally (§5, "Witness points and the contact").

    2Support functions and the Minkowski difference

    A support function answers one question: for a direction d, which point of the shape is furthest along d?

    s_A(d) = \arg\max_{x \in A} \; d \cdot x

    For a convex shape this answer is enough to describe the whole shape: the shape is the intersection of all half-spaces \{x : d\cdot x \le d \cdot s_A(d)\}. GJK and EPA never look at faces, radii or meshes directly; they only call support(). That is what makes one algorithm work for every convex type. Most cases in collision_gjk.py::support first rotate d into the geom frame (local_dir = transpose(rot) @ dir), pick the extreme point there, and rotate back; SPHERE, HFIELD, TRIANGLE and FLEX work with the world direction directly:

    GeomSupport point in the geom frame (size = geom_size)Behaviour as d turns
    SPHERE(r + margin/2) · dir (world frame, returns early)slides smoothly
    BOXsign(local_dir) ⊙ size; vertex_index = corner bits (x→1, y→2, z→4)jumps corner to corner
    CAPSULEr · local_dir + sign(local_dir.z) · halflength · ẑsmooth on a cap, jumps between caps
    ELLIPSOIDnormalize(local_dir ⊙ size) ⊙ sizesmooth
    CYLINDERrim point r · (dx, dy)/|(dx, dy)|, z = ±halfheight by the sign of dzslides on a rim, jumps between rims
    MESHthe vertex with the largest dot(v, local_dir): exhaustive below 10 vertices, else hill-climbing (§8)jumps vertex to vertex
    HFIELD, FLEX, TRIANGLEthe best of the prism's 6 vertices (HFIELD), the triangle's 3 (TRIANGLE) or the flex element's 4 (FLEX); then the common margin shift (Ch 18)jumps

    After the type branch, every non-sphere support point is pushed out by 0.5 * geom.margin along dir. Each geom of the pair gets half of the pair's margin, so the two inflated shapes touch exactly when the real ones are margin apart. Play with Figure 2: drag the direction slider or press Play, switch the shape, and watch how the support point slides on round shapes and jumps on flat ones.

    The Minkowski difference

    Two shapes A and B overlap exactly when some point a \in A equals some point b \in B, that is when a - b = 0. Collect all such differences into one set, the Minkowski difference:

    A \ominus B = \{\, a - b : a \in A,\; b \in B \,\}

    Three facts make it the central object of this chapter:

    • It is convex when A and B are.
    • A and B overlap if and only if the origin lies inside A \ominus B; their distance is the distance from the origin to A \ominus B; the penetration depth is the distance from the origin to its boundary.
    • Its support function is free: s_{A\ominus B}(d) = s_A(d) - s_B(-d). That is exactly what MJWarp computes in _gjk_support (sp1 = support(geom1, -dir), sp2 = support(geom2, dir)) and _epa_support.

    So the 3-D question "do these two shapes touch, and how deep?" becomes a question about one convex set and one point: "where is the origin relative to A \ominus B?" Figure 3 shows the construction for two polygons.

    Example 17.2 writes the support functions of four geom types in NumPy, following support() line by line, and checks each one against brute force: the maximum of dot(x, dir) over 20,000 surface samples. The shapes come from an MjSpec (Ch 2 §11), so the sizes follow MuJoCo's geom_size convention (capsule: radius, half-length).

    The box error is zero because the corner is exact; the smooth shapes show the sampling error of the brute force, not an error of the formula. The margin line confirms the margin/2 shift, and the last line is the Minkowski support: along +x the difference "sphere minus box" reaches only to x = -0.05, so the origin is outside and the shapes are 5 cm apart.

    Pitfall: unnormalized directions

    support() assumes dir is a unit vector: the sphere and capsule branches scale it by the radius, and the margin shift is dir * margin/2. GJK and EPA always pass a unit direction (x_k / |x_k| or a normalized face normal); only the heightfield witness fix-ups (_epa_witness, _inflate) pass a raw point. If you call the support of a sphere or capsule from your own code, normalize first.

    3GJK: walking a simplex towards the origin

    GJK answers "how far is the origin from A \ominus B?" without ever building A \ominus B. It keeps a small simplex (1 to 4 support points of A \ominus B) and a current guess x_k, the point of that simplex closest to the origin. Each iteration does four things:

    1. Ask for a new point. The support point of A \ominus B in the direction -x_k (towards the origin) is s_k. In code: _gjk_support returns sp1 = support(geom1, -dir) and sp2 = support(geom2, dir) with dir = x_k / |x_k|, and simplex[n] = sp1.point - sp2.point.
    2. Test for convergence. If s_k is no closer to the origin than x_k already is, nothing better exists and GJK stops (the duality gap, below).
    3. Test for separation. If x_k \cdot s_k > 0, the plane through the origin with normal x_k has the whole of A \ominus B on one side: the shapes are certainly apart.
    4. Shrink the simplex. Add s_k, find the closest point of the new simplex to the origin (the distance sub-algorithm), drop the vertices that do not contribute, and set x_{k+1} to that point. If the simplex became a full tetrahedron that contains the origin, the shapes overlap.

    The stopping rule: a Frank-Wolfe duality gap

    Write f(x) = \tfrac12 |x|^2. GJK is a Frank-Wolfe method for minimizing f over the convex set A \ominus B: the support point s_k minimizes the linearization \nabla f(x_k)\cdot s = x_k \cdot s. Convexity gives a free certificate of how far from optimal we are:

    f(x_k) - f(x^\star) \;\le\; x_k \cdot (x_k - s_k) \;=\; \text{gap}_k

    gjk() stops when dot(x_dir, x_dir - simplex[n]) < epsilon with epsilon = 0.5 * tolerance**2 (here x_dir is x_k, re-aimed by _gjk_support for discrete pairs (box/mesh/hfield on both sides, no margin) when |x_k| < 1e-4: perpendicular to the edge (n = 2) or along the triangle normal (n = 3)). For two discrete shapes (box, mesh or heightfield prism on both sides, no margin; _discrete_geoms) the support set is finite, GJK terminates exactly, and epsilon = 0: the tolerance is ignored. The other exits are |x_k| < min_norm (the origin is on the simplex), a stalled norm (|xnorm_prev - xnorm| < MINVAL) and running out of gjk_iterations, which is m.opt.ccd_iterations (§7).

    The separation cutoff

    The value x_k\cdot s_k / |x_k| is a lower bound on the distance. If the caller only cares about contacts closer than some cutoff, GJK can quit as soon as that bound is beyond it: if lower > 0 and lower >= cutoff * xnorm: separated. The CCD kernel passes cutoff = gap (the pair's geom_gap, usually 0; a collision sensor passes 1e32 to get real distances), and with cutoff == 0 the test is simply lower > 0. Clearly separated pairs therefore often cost only one or two support calls.

    The signed-volume distance sub-algorithm (_S1D, _S2D, _S3D)

    The heart of GJK is "closest point of a simplex to the origin, and which vertices does it use?". The classic answer tests Voronoi regions one by one. MJWarp uses the signed-volume method of Montanari et al., which works with determinants only and is robust in float32:

    • _S3D(s1, s2, s3, s4) computes the cofactors C41..C44 of the 4x4 matrix \begin{bmatrix} s_1 & s_2 & s_3 & s_4 \\ 1 & 1 & 1 & 1\end{bmatrix}. Their sum m_det is 6 times the signed volume of the tetrahedron, and each C4i is the signed volume of the tetrahedron with vertex i replaced by the origin. If all four have the sign of m_det, the origin is inside and the barycentric weights are C4i / m_det. Otherwise every face whose cofactor has the wrong sign is a candidate; _S3D calls _S2D on each and keeps the one with the smallest |x|.
    • _S2D(s1, s2, s3) projects the origin onto the triangle's plane (_project_origin_plane), drops the coordinate axis along which the triangle's shadow is largest (minors M_14, M_24, M_34), and repeats the same sign test with 2-D signed areas C31..C33. Inside: three weights. Outside: the best edge by _S1D.
    • _S1D(s1, s2) projects the origin onto the line (_project_origin_line) and tests the sign along the axis with the largest extent. Inside: two weights; outside: (0, 1), keep the newer vertex.

    The weights that come back are the barycentric coordinates lmbda. gjk() keeps the vertices with lmbda[i] != 0, and the same weights applied to the per-shape points simplex1 and simplex2 give the two witness points x1 and x2. In w0 the teaching GJK finds 0.02000, the 2 cm we built in, and MJWarp writes two contacts with exactly that dist: box B has margin="0.05", so pairs up to 5 cm apart become contacts, and the vertical edge of the turned box faces the flat side of A, so multi-contact writes the two ends of that edge.     Why the origin shift?

    eval_ccd_write_contact moves the pair so that geom1.pos = 0 before calling GJK, and adds origin back when it writes contact.pos. In float32 a pair at x = 1000 m would otherwise keep only about 0.06 mm of resolution in every support point. The comment in the kernel: "Shift origin to geom1 position for translation invariance with numerical precision."

    4gjk_phase: shrink, run GJK, inflate back

    A sphere is a point with a radius; a capsule is a segment with a radius. GJK on a point or a segment is cheap and exact (the support set is finite), while GJK on the round shape converges only within the tolerance. gjk_phase exploits this: it shrinks every sphere and capsule to its core (geom.size[0] = 0, geom.margin = 0), remembers how much it removed in full_margin = size[0] + 0.5 * margin, and runs GJK on the cores with the cutoff enlarged by the same amount. For a pair of faceted shapes that can take part in multi-contact (_supports_multiccd) it does the same with the margin alone (full_margin = 0.5 * margin).

    • Cores apart by more than tolerance (result.dist > tolerance): the real shapes either touch shallowly or not at all. _inflate moves the witness points back out along n = (x_2 - x_1)/|x_2 - x_1| by full_margin1 and full_margin2, and dist -= full_margin1 + full_margin2. A negative result is a penetration depth, found without EPA.
    • Cores within tolerance of touching: the cores themselves overlap (deep penetration), and shrinking no longer helps. gjk_phase restores the full sizes and margins, reruns GJK on the full shapes and, if the result is "overlapping with a simplex of at least 2 points", returns needs_epa = True.
    • No sphere, capsule or eligible margin: GJK runs once on the full shapes, and EPA is needed when dist <= tolerance, dim >= 2 and the shapes are not separated.
    PairShrunk in gjk_phaseTypical outcome
    capsule-ellipsoid, capsule-cylinder, capsule-meshcapsule to its segmentshallow: one GJK, no EPA
    sphere-ellipsoid, sphere-meshsphere to its centre pointshallow: one GJK, no EPA
    box-box, box-mesh, cylinder-box (margin > 0)margin onlyseparated in the margin band: GJK; penetrating: EPA
    ellipsoid-box, ellipsoid-cylinder (any margin)nothingpenetrating: GJK + EPA

    Deep dive: why "tolerance" decides

    When the cores are apart by more than ccd_tolerance (default 1e-6; per world, opt_ccd_tolerance[worldid % ...]), the inflated distance is exact up to that tolerance, because the closest points of a rounded shape are the closest points of its core moved along the same normal. Below it, the normal x_2 - x_1 is undefined (the cores touch), so the phase falls back to the full shapes and EPA.

    5EPA: how deep, and in which direction

    When GJK ends with the origin inside A \ominus B, the penetration depth is the distance from the origin to the boundary of A \ominus B, and the contact normal is the direction of that closest boundary point. EPA finds it by growing a polytope inside A \ominus B, always pushing out the face nearest to the origin, until that face lies on the boundary.

    Step 0: a polytope that contains the origin

    EPA needs a closed polytope around the origin. GJK may have ended with 2, 3 or 4 points (it can also stop early when the origin sits on an edge or a face), so epa_phase picks one of three builders:

    The EPA loop (_epa)

    Every face stores face_pr, the projection of the origin onto its plane (pointing outward, made sure of by comparing with pt.center in _attach_face), and face_norm2 = |face_pr|^2. Then, up to epa_iterations times:

    1. Lower bound. The valid face with the smallest face_norm2: no point of the boundary can be closer than its plane, so lower = sqrt(lower2).
    2. Upper bound. The support point w of A \ominus B along that face's normal (_epa_support); upper_k = dot(face_pr, w) / lower, and upper = min(upper, upper_k).
    3. Stop when upper - lower < epsilon (ccd_tolerance, or MIN_EPATOL = 1e-7 for discrete shapes, which also stop when w repeats a known vertex pair).
    4. Expand. Delete the closest face and every face w can see (dot(face_pr[i], w) - face_norm2[i] > 1e-10). Their boundary is the horizon. Attach one new face from w to every horizon edge. New faces whose distance is outside [lower2, upper2] cannot be the answer and are marked invalid at birth.

    The horizon is collected by _add_edge with a neat trick: each deleted face adds its three edges, and an edge that is already in the list is removed instead (it was shared by two deleted faces, so it is interior). What remains is exactly the loop. Figure 8 runs the loop in 2-D, where faces are edges and the horizon is two vertices.

    The two bounds squeeze the answer from both sides: in the deep case lower climbs 0.027 → 0.509 → 0.599 while upper falls 1.43 → 0.65 → 0.599. Each iteration adds exactly one vertex. Each vertex takes 2 slots (one point per shape: pt.vert[2*i] on geom 1, pt.vert[2*i+1] on geom 2), and the start polytope has up to 5 vertices, which is why the scratch row holds 2 * (5 + epa_iterations) = 10 + 2 * epa_iterations vertex slots (§7). For polygons EPA is exact; on curved shapes it only approximates the boundary with flat faces, and the number of iterations sets the error (Example 17.7).

    EPA in 3-D, checked against MJWarp

    Example 17.6 ports _epa, _attach_face and _add_edge to NumPy and compares with MJWarp on two boxes: axis-aligned (EPA converges at once) and tilted (six iterations). Step through the tilted case in Figure 9: the visible faces turn red, the horizon loop is orange, and a fan of new faces closes the hole.

    The depth agrees to five digits in both worlds. MJWarp writes 4 contacts in w0 and 2 in w1 where the teaching EPA has one depth: that is multi-contact at work (§6). The deepest contact (-0.04196) is the EPA answer; the second one is a clipped point of the touching faces. Notice also how the polytope grows: one new vertex per iteration, and deleting f faces opens a horizon of f + 2 edges, so the number of live faces grows by a net +2 (the faces= column counts only those). The storage grows faster: _epa never reuses the slot of a deleted face, it only sets _FACE_DELETED_BIT, and _attach_face appends every new face at pt.nface. Each iteration therefore writes f + 2 (at least 3) new face slots; in w1 the 4 start faces plus 4 + 3 + 4 + 4 + 3 new ones fill 22 slots for 14 live faces. The 6 + MJ_MAX_EPAFACES * epa_iterations slots (MJ_MAX_EPAFACES = 5) allow an average horizon of up to 5 edges per iteration. When the face array is full, _attach_face returns 0 and EPA stops without a contact and without setting an overflow bit, unlike a horizon of more than 24 edges, which sets OverflowType.EPA_HORIZON. Both only happen when support points keep seeing a large part of the polytope at once (very flat or very fine polytopes).

    Face bit packing

    Faces live in one int each. _attach_face writes face = v1 + (v2 << 10) + (v3 << 20): three 10-bit vertex indices (so at most 1024 vertices, which is why _epa caps epa_iterations at 1000). The top two bits are flags: _FACE_DELETED_BIT = 0x80000000 (removed from the polytope) and _FACE_INVALID_BIT = 0x40000000 (still part of the polytope, but never a candidate). Horizon edges pack the same way: (min(e1, e2) << 10) | max(e1, e2), so (3, 7) and (7, 3) are the same number and cancel in _add_edge.

    Witness points and the contact

    EPA's answer is a face of the polytope. _epa_witness computes the affine coordinates of face_pr in that triangle (_tri_affine_coord) and applies them to the per-shape vertices (pt.vert[2*i] on geom 1, pt.vert[2*i+1] on geom 2), exactly like GJK's lmbda. It returns x1, x2 and dist = -sqrt(face_norm2). The kernel then writes pos = 0.5 * (x1 + x2) + origin and the normal frame = make_frame(x1 - x2) (for penetration, x1 has crossed past x2, so this points from geom[0] to geom[1], the convention of Ch 16). For a contact that is still separated inside the margin band (dist > margin after the correction below), x2 - x1 is used instead, so the normal still points from geom[0] to geom[1].

    The distance needs one correction. support() inflates each geom by 0.5 * margin, so GJK and EPA measure the distance between the inflated shapes. eval_ccd_write_contact drops the pair when that inflated dist >= gap (the inflated shapes are more than gap apart, which is the same as a true distance of at least margin + gap), and then does dist += margin before write_contact, so CCD contacts report the true surface-to-surface distance, like the primitive colliders of Ch 16.

    Version: dev tree vs 4.0.1

    At 8f5b9f1 the dev tree's _epa_witness adds a fallback (EPA_WITNESS_TOL = 1e-3): when coplanar faces tie and the chosen face does not contain the witness, it searches for the face the ray along the normal exits. The 4.0.1 wheel uses the selected face as is. Depths and normals are the same; the contact position can differ on flat-against-flat ties.

    6Multi-contact: from one witness pair to four contacts

    GJK and EPA return one pair of witness points. That is enough for a ball, but a box resting flat on a table with a single contact would balance on one point and rock. multicontact recovers the whole touching patch:

    1. Feature dimension. _feature_dim looks at the EPA face's three vertices on each shape (via the vertex_index that support() recorded: box corner bits, mesh vertex ids, cylinder cap 0/1). Three distinct ids = a face (dim 3), two = an edge (dim 2), one = a vertex.
    2. Candidate normals. _box_normals, _mesh_normals, _cylinder_normals list the face normals around that feature.
    3. Aligned faces. _aligned_faces looks for one normal of each shape pointing against each other within FACE_TOL = cos(0.0889) = 0.99605 (about 5.1 degrees). Failing that, _aligned_face_edge tries an edge perpendicular to a face within EDGE_TOL = sin(0.0889) = 0.08878. No match: keep the single EPA contact.
    4. Clip. _box_face / _mesh_face / _cylinder_face (a 16-gon, _CYLINDER_COS_16) give the two polygons; _polygon_clip clips one against the side planes of the other (Sutherland-Hodgman), drops points farther than the margin from the face, and if more than 4 remain, _polygon_quad keeps the quadrilateral of maximum area.

    When does it run? The kernel builder decides at compile time:

    # collision_convex.py::ccd_kernel_builder (eval_ccd_write_contact), 4.0.1
if wp.static(
  (geomtype1 == GeomType.BOX and geomtype2 == GeomType.BOX)
  or (use_multiccd and ((has_face1 and has_face2) or (has_edge1 and has_face2) or (has_face1 and has_edge2)))
):

    Faces are box, mesh and cylinder; a capsule is an edge. So box-box is always multi-contact, while every other face/face or edge/face pair is multi-contact unless DisableBit.MULTICCD is set (<flag multiccd="disable"/>). Spheres and ellipsoids never are. Multi-contact also needs a scratch row: if GJK alone found the (shallow) contact, the kernel reserves one and converts the GJK simplex into an EPA face with _gjk_simplex_to_epa. A mesh without polygon data (mesh_polyadr < 0) skips it.

    Flat, every pair touches face to face and gets 4 contacts, unless MULTICCD is disabled: then only box-box keeps its 4 and the cylinder and mesh fall back to 1 (and would rock). Tilted by 20 degrees, the box rests on an edge (2 contacts: an edge clipped against a face), while the cylinder and the hexagonal prism touch with a single rim point or corner, for which one contact is the right answer.

    Pitfall: disabling MULTICCD for speed

    Disabling the flag shrinks the multi-contact scratch and skips the clipping, but cylinders, meshes, and capsules lying on a cylinder or mesh then get one contact each and wobble or slowly spin. Box-box is unaffected. Measure the stability of your scene before you trade it for throughput.

    7The kernels and their scratch memory

    One kernel per convex type pair, grid-stride over all candidates

    convex_narrowphase loops over the CONVEX entries of the collision table and, for every type pair that occurs in the model (m.geom_pair_type_count), builds a kernel with ccd_kernel_builder(g1, g2, gjk_iterations, epa_iterations, use_multiccd, geomgeomid, block_dim, warn_overflow). Everything in that list is a compile-time constant (@cache_kernel), so changing ccd_iterations or the MULTICCD flag builds new kernels. Heightfield pairs use ccd_hfield_kernel_builder (Ch 18).

    Each kernel is a grid-stride loop: layout 4 of the launch-shape atlas (owned by this chapter, Figure 12; the atlas is introduced in Ch 7).

    # collision_convex.py::ccd_kernel, 4.0.1
tid = wp.tid()
for collisionid in range(tid, wp.min(ncollision_in[0], naconmax_in), grid_stride_in):
  geoms = collision_pair_in[collisionid]
  if geom_type[g1] != geomtype1 or geom_type[g2] != geomtype2:
    continue                         # another kernel's pair type
  ...
  eval_ccd_write_contact(...)        # gjk_phase, epa_phase, multicontact, write_contact

    Why grid-stride here and not one thread per slot?

    A CCD thread is heavy (registers for GJK, EPA and clipping), and most of the naconmax slots are empty or hold another pair type. The comment on _ccd_grid_size: "we avoid launching one (mostly idle) thread per naconmax slot". The kernel also sets launch_bounds=(block_dim, _CCD_MIN_BLOCKS) with block_dim = m.block_dim.convex_ccd (default 64) so the compiler keeps enough blocks resident.

    Scratch: one naccdmax-row block, a row budget per pair type

    EPA and clipping need arrays (polytope vertices, faces, horizon, polygons). They cannot live in registers, so convex_narrowphase allocates them in global memory with wp.empty(shape=(d.naccdmax, ...)), and each pair that needs them takes a row: ccdid = wp.atomic_add(nccd, geomgeomid, 1). The counter is per type pair, so naccdmax is the number of EPA/multi-contact pairs one pair-type kernel can handle in one collision call, over all worlds. Past it, the pair is dropped and the world gets the OverflowType.CCD bit ("CCD overflow - please increase naccdmax beyond %u"). make_data defaults naccdmax to naconmax and refuses a larger value. The arrays are allocated once per convex_narrowphase call and the pair-type kernels run one after another, so they reuse the same rows; each kernel counts its own rows from 0 in nccd[geomgeomid]. The memory is one block of naccdmax rows however many convex pair types the model has.

    Like nconmax / naconmax, the budget also has a per-world form: mjw.make_data(mjm, nworld=N, nconmax=48, nccdmax=8) (also on put_data) sets naccdmax = nccdmax * nworld, so it scales when you change nworld between a CPU test and a GPU run. An explicit naccdmax overrides it, and make_data requires nccdmax <= nconmax. Example 17.8 uses nccdmax = 2 with 2 worlds.

    The row size follows rules in convex_narrowphase (4.0.1, lines ~1279-1324 and ~1418-1438):

    • epa_iterations = 16 if every convex pair in the model is box-box, else m.opt.ccd_iterations.
    • EPA arrays: 10 + 2*epa_iterations vertices (epa_vert vec3 + epa_vert_index int), 6 + MJ_MAX_EPAFACES*epa_iterations faces (epa_face int, epa_pr vec3, epa_norm2 float), MJ_MAX_EPAHORIZON = 24 horizon ints.
    • Multi-contact arrays sized by npolygonmax (4 with box-box; at least 16 with cylinder pairs; m.npolygonmax with meshes) and nmeshdegmax (3, or m.nmeshdegmax).

    Part 1 shows the failure mode: 12 ellipsoids sink into 12 boxes, every pair needs EPA, only 4 rows exist, and 8 contacts vanish. Both worlds get the CCD bit even though the rows are shared, because whichever thread came too late sets the bit of its world. Part 2 confirms the sizing rules byte for byte. On a GPU with nworld = 4096 and nconmax = 48, the default naccdmax = naconmax means about 1 GiB of scratch for the mesh row size, allocated and freed in every collision call in 4.0.1: setting naccdmax to what your scene really needs (count EPA pairs, see the pitfall below) saves memory.

    Pitfall: sizing naccdmax by contacts

    One CCD row can produce up to 4 contacts. Shallow (non-penetrating) sphere pairs and capsule-ellipsoid pairs need no row. Every box-box pair in contact (dist < margin) takes a row, and with MULTICCD on so does every face/face or capsule-edge/face pair in contact (box, cylinder or mesh against each other, capsule against cylinder or mesh), even without EPA. Count pairs that penetrate or take multi-contact, per convex type pair, over all worlds, not contacts. Then size it per world with nccdmax (the count per world, plus a safety factor), so it follows nworld. If in doubt, watch d.overflow & mjw.OverflowType.CCD during a representative rollout and keep a safety factor.

    Version: dev tree vs 4.0.1

    In the 4.0.1 wheel the EPA and multi-contact arrays are allocated inline in convex_narrowphase on every call. The dev tree at 8f5b9f1 moves them into prepare_convex_context / _convex_scratch_dims (stored on the collision context, allocated once) and adds a multiccd_edge array. Dev tree only, not in 4.0.1: do not call these names.

    Tuning ccd_iterations and ccd_tolerance

    m.opt.ccd_iterations (MJCF <option ccd_iterations=.../>, default 35) bounds the GJK loop and, unless the model is box-box only, the EPA loop. ccd_tolerance (default 1e-6) is the GJK and EPA stopping tolerance for non-discrete shapes; it is a per-world batched array, while ccd_iterations is a Python int that is baked into the kernel. Example 17.7 sinks a cylinder's round side into the ridge of a box by 5, 15 and 30 mm and compares the depth with the exact value.

    With 4 iterations the shallow world loses its contact entirely: GJK stopped after 4 support points without enclosing the origin, so gjk_phase saw a positive distance, skipped EPA, and the kernel dropped the pair (dist >= gap). The deeper worlds get contacts, but EPA had too few faces to approximate the round side: errors of 4-5 mm, more than 10% of the depth. 8 iterations bring the error under 0.6 mm; 35 makes it vanish. The cost grows linearly with the iterations actually used (most pairs converge early) and the scratch row grows with ccd_iterations through epa_iterations.

    Tip: what to tune

    Keep the default 35 unless profiling shows CCD dominates (Ch 25). Box-only scenes already use 16 EPA iterations. Lowering ccd_iterations mostly hurts curved shapes (cylinders, ellipsoids) in deep contact; raising ccd_tolerance mostly saves GJK iterations on curved pairs. Read d.overflow for CCD and EPA_HORIZON after any change.

    8Meshes: convex hulls and hill-climbing

    The MESH branch of support() returns the vertex with the largest dot(v, local_dir). For fewer than 10 vertices (or no graph) it scans them all. Otherwise it hill-climbs on geom.graph, the adjacency of the mesh's convex hull that MuJoCo computes at compile time (mesh_graphadr): start at the vertex the previous call returned (geom.index, cached in cached_index between GJK iterations), scan all neighbours of the current vertex and move to the one with the largest dot product (if it beats the current vertex), and stop when no neighbour improves. On a convex polytope a local maximum of a linear function is the global one, so hill-climbing is exact, and with the warm start it usually takes only a few steps.

    The consequence: a mesh always collides as its convex hull. GJK and EPA only see support points, and the support function of a point set is the support function of its hull. A bowl collides like a solid puck, an L-shaped bracket like a triangle-ish slab. Example 17.10 drops a ball into the notch of an L-shaped block.

    The ball stops at z = 0.1496, 0.4 mm below the predicted 0.1500 (the usual soft-contact penetration), on a lid that does not exist. To get a real notch, split the mesh into convex pieces (for example with V-HACD or CoACD) and attach each piece as its own geom; MuJoCo C behaves the same way, so the decomposition carries over.

    Version: dev tree vs 4.0.1

    The dev tree's hill-climb loads four neighbours at a time ("so their dependent loads overlap"); 4.0.1 walks the neighbour list one entry at a time. Same result, different memory latency.

    def gjk2d_witness(A, B, iterations=20, tol=1e-6):
    x_k = A.mean(0) - B.mean(0)
    simplex, simplex1, simplex2 = [], [], []
    lam = np.ones(1)
    for k in range(iterations):
        xnorm = np.linalg.norm(x_k)
        if xnorm < tol:
            break
        d = x_k / xnorm
        p1, p2 = support_poly(A, -d), support_poly(B, d)
        s = p1 - p2
        if x_k @ (x_k - s) < 0.5 * tol * tol:
            break
        simplex.append(s); simplex1.append(p1); simplex2.append(p2)
        if len(simplex) == 1:
            lam, inside = np.array([1.0]), False
        elif len(simplex) == 2:
            lam, inside = s1d(*simplex), False
        else:
            lam, inside = s2d(*simplex)
        keep = [i for i in range(len(simplex)) if lam[i] != 0.0]
        simplex = [simplex[i] for i in keep]
        simplex1 = [simplex1[i] for i in keep]
        simplex2 = [simplex2[i] for i in keep]
        lam = lam[keep]
        x_k = sum(l * p for l, p in zip(lam, simplex))
        if inside:
            break
    x1 = sum(l * p for l, p in zip(lam, simplex1))   # same weights, applied to each shape
    x2 = sum(l * p for l, p in zip(lam, simplex2))
    return float(np.linalg.norm(x_k)), x1, x2

    def cylinder_support(size, pos, rot, dir):
    """Mirror of the CYLINDER branch of collision_gjk.py::support (margin 0)."""
    local = rot.T @ (dir / np.linalg.norm(dir))
    res = np.zeros(3)
    d = np.hypot(local[0], local[1])
    if d > MINVAL:                                  # point on the rim circle
        res[:2] = local[:2] * size[0] / d
    res[2] = size[1] if local[2] >= 0.0 else -size[1]   # top or bottom cap
    return rot @ res + pos

    The d > MINVAL guard matters for dir = ±z: any rim point is a support point, and the code returns the cap centre, which has the same dot product. Output: 8 directions pass.

    11Summary

    Key takeaways

    • Every convex pair that is not PRIMITIVE goes through one path: support functions → GJK → (EPA) → (multi-contact) → write_contact. There is no MPR.
    • A support function is all a shape contributes; the Minkowski difference's support is support(A, d) - support(B, -d), and the origin is inside it exactly when the shapes overlap.
    • GJK shrinks a simplex towards the origin with the signed-volume sub-algorithm (_S1D/_S2D/_S3D), stops on the Frank-Wolfe duality gap (exactly for discrete shapes), and quits early past the cutoff.
    • gjk_phase shrinks spheres and capsules to their cores and inflates the answer back; only deep penetration needs EPA.
    • EPA grows a polytope from _polytope2/3/4, squeezes the depth between a lower and an upper bound, and rebuilds around the horizon found by _add_edge; faces are 3 x 10-bit indices plus deleted/invalid bits, and horizons longer than 24 set EPA_HORIZON.
    • Multi-contact clips aligned faces (Sutherland-Hodgman) and keeps the max-area 4 points; it is always on for box-box and controlled by MULTICCD otherwise.
    • One grid-stride kernel per convex type pair; EPA/multi-contact pairs take rows of one shared naccdmax-row scratch block (budget counted per pair type; CCD overflow when exhausted); ccd_iterations is compiled in and sizes the rows.
    • Meshes collide as their convex hulls (hill-climbing on the hull graph); decompose non-convex meshes.

    Next, Ch 18 applies the same CCD machinery to heightfield prisms and then leaves it for signed distance fields.
