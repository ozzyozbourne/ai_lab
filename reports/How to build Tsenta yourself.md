# Build your own Tsenta, skip the bans

Tsenta is a two-person YC S26 startup selling an AI agent that **finds jobs on employer career pages, tailors a resume per role, fills and submits the ATS form from cloud browsers, and tracks replies by reading your Gmail or Outlook**. It claims roughly **$3M annualized revenue and 90,000+ job seekers** within a few months. Those figures are self-reported, and third-party estimates disagree with them. You can build a personal version in a few weekends from parts that are mostly public. Greenhouse, Lever and Ashby publish free JSON job feeds and form schemas. LLM structured extraction handles resume parsing and form mapping, open-source projects (Resume-Matcher, RenderCV) cover tailoring and rendering, and Playwright drives the browser. Done well, LLM cost is about **one to five cents per application**. The hard parts are elsewhere: Workday's per-employer account wizard, reCAPTCHA and bot detection at submit time, and a legal and platform environment where LinkedIn bans automation outright and ATS vendors now run fraud detection on applicants. The approach that holds up is to run locally in your own browser, cover the main ATSs with fixed per-site scripts and use an LLM only for the rest, review every submission before it goes out, and never automate LinkedIn. For a product, the same core gets cloud workers, a credential vault, Google's CASA security assessment and much stricter controls against invented answers. Throughout this report, "stated" means Tsenta's own pages say it, "vendor" means a party with a commercial interest, and "inferred" means engineering judgment, not a source.

## Tsenta is a four-stage pipeline that moved from desktop to cloud

Tsenta describes itself as "a Y Combinator-backed (S26) AI agent that applies to jobs for you and tracks every application," organized as **Find, Prep, Apply, Track** ([tsenta.com](https://tsenta.com); [llms.txt](https://tsenta.com/llms.txt)). In **Find**, it says it watches **300,000+ company career pages** and ranks new roles against your resume rather than by keyword ([tsenta.com](https://tsenta.com)). That number has grown with each telling: the YC launch cited "over 2 million jobs, updated every couple of hours," and a March 2026 profile cited 1M+ listings ([YC Launch](https://www.ycombinator.com/launches/SBV-tsenta-your-ai-career-agent-that-applies-to-jobs-lands-you-interviews); [HUGE Magazine](https://huge-magazine.pages.dev/feature/tsenta)). In **Prep**, it pulls must-haves and disqualifiers from the job description, rewrites bullets and drafts a cover letter when the form asks for one. Three tailoring modes are offered: Off, Honest ("rewords real experience") and Aggressive, which "may stretch claims" ([Docs: profile and resume](https://docs.tsenta.com/product/profile-and-resume.md)). That last mode is a liability you should not copy. In **Apply**, it "logs in to supported company career sites, fills every field, drafts open-ended answers in your voice, uploads documents, and submits," all "in Tsenta's cloud, so no device needs to stay on" ([tsenta.com](https://tsenta.com)). In **Track**, it reads job-related mail through the `gmail.readonly` and `gmail.compose` scopes. It matches recruiter replies to applications, enters verification codes during applications and saves reply drafts without sending them ([Privacy](https://tsenta.com/privacy)).

Users control the automation through three independent switches. **Resume auto-approval** (off by default) pauses so you can review a diff of the tailored resume. **Review before submit** pauses a filled form so you can check answers, but only on compatible systems. **Auto Apply**, on the $99 Power plan only, submits with no review, governed by filters, a daily cap and a match threshold ([Docs: how applications work](https://docs.tsenta.com/product/how-auto-apply-works.md)). The public agent API exposes the state machine behind these switches: `apply-to-job`, then `PENDING_RESUME_REVIEW`, then `approve-application`, then `PENDING_SUBMIT_REVIEW`, then `submit-application-review` ([Docs: agent quickstart](https://docs.tsenta.com/product/agent-quickstart.md)). Tsenta offers the same tools as a remote MCP server at `api.autojobs.me/api/v1/mcp`, usable from Claude, ChatGPT and Cursor ([Docs: MCP connector](https://docs.tsenta.com/product/mcp-connector.md)). Other surfaces are a web dashboard, iOS and Android apps, iMessage and WhatsApp, and a Chrome extension that captures a role from LinkedIn or Indeed and hands it to the cloud workflow ([Docs: apps and surfaces](https://docs.tsenta.com/product/apps-and-surfaces.md)).

The architecture has changed recently. A March 2026 profile described "AI-native, on-device job automation": a Mac, Windows and Linux desktop app running browser automation locally, which it said made the automation harder for anti-bot systems to detect ([HUGE Magazine](https://huge-magazine.pages.dev/feature/tsenta)). Current docs say flatly that "the native desktop app is retired" ([Docs: apps and surfaces](https://docs.tsenta.com/product/apps-and-surfaces.md)). The likely trade, which is inferred, was detection resistance and zero compute cost given up for always-on runs, mobile and messaging use, and Auto Apply at 150 applications a day. The privacy policy lists the vendors: **Anthropic, OpenAI and Azure AI Foundry** for LLMs, **OVHcloud** for hosting, **Neon Postgres**, Cloudflare, Firebase Auth, PostHog, Sentry, Resend/SES, and **Composio** for legacy Gmail and all Outlook connections ([Privacy](https://tsenta.com/privacy)). Pricing is 25 free applications, then **$19 for 600, $39 for 1,500 or $99 for 4,500 per 30 days**, charged only on successful submission, or about two to three cents per application ([tsenta.com](https://tsenta.com)). That price point implies Tsenta's combined LLM and browser cost per application is well under three cents. That is inferred, but it tells you which architecture it must be running: fixed per-site scripts with an LLM writing one fill plan per form, not a screenshot agent. Two things are not public: the list of supported ATSs (the launch claimed 30+, including "account creation" and OTP handling) and anything about CAPTCHAs, proxies or credential storage ([YC Launch](https://www.ycombinator.com/launches/SBV-tsenta-your-ai-career-agent-that-applies-to-jobs-lands-you-interviews); [Docs: supported systems](https://docs.tsenta.com/product/supported-job-boards.md)).

## Six components do the work, and public ATS endpoints supply the data

A Tsenta clone breaks into six components: job discovery, profile, matching, tailoring, the apply engine, and tracking. Only the apply engine is truly hard. The table below gives the recommended choice for each, followed by the reasoning.

| Component | Recommended build | Why / evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Job discovery | Poll Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby, SmartRecruiters public JSON feeds and Workday's `cxs` endpoint against a list of company slugs (each employer's account name on that ATS); paid aggregator for the long tail | Unauthenticated, fresh, carry real apply URLs ([Greenhouse](https://docs.greenhouse.io/job-board.html); [Lever](https://github.com/lever/postings-api); [Ashby](https://developers.ashbyhq.com/docs/public-job-posting-api)) |
| Profile | PDF to text (pymupdf/pdfplumber), then an LLM with constrained JSON output into a JSON Resume schema plus work authorization, salary, EEO and saved Q&A, every bullet given a stable ID | JSON Resume v1.7.1 covers most sections ([jsonresume.org](https://jsonresume.org/schema)); vendor parser accuracy claims are unaudited ([Affinda](https://www.affinda.com/resume-parser-for-job-boards)) |
| Matching | SQL hard filters, then hybrid search (Postgres full-text search plus pgvector, merged by reciprocal rank fusion with k≈60), then an LLM re-rank of the top 20 that returns a 0–100 score and evidence | Supabase pattern ([Supabase](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/ai/hybrid-search)); the re-rank adds about 5 to 9 nDCG points over retrieval alone ([arXiv 2605.09760](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.09760)) |
| Tailoring | LLM selects and rephrases existing bullets, citing source IDs, followed by a programmatic fact check; render with RenderCV (Typst) or Playwright HTML→PDF; re-extract the text to confirm an ATS can parse it | Resume-Matcher's design, whose guard is a prompt only ([Resume-Matcher](https://github.com/srbhr/Resume-Matcher)); [RenderCV](https://github.com/rendercv/rendercv) |
| Apply engine | Fixed Playwright script per ATS, then a generic LLM agent that works from the accessibility tree and caches selectors (Stagehand/browser-use), then a vision computer-use fallback | Hand-written scripts score ~98% versus 75–89% for LLM agents in one non-comparable roundup ([noqta.tn](https://noqta.tn/blog/ai-browser-agents-browser-use-stagehand-web-automation-2026)) |
| Tracking | Gmail `users.watch` → Pub/Sub → `history.list`, with a cheap LLM classifier for confirmation, rejection and interview emails | [Gmail push guide](https://developers.google.com/workspace/gmail/api/guides/push) |

The most useful fact for discovery is that the major ATSs publish what you need for free. `GET boards-api.greenhouse.io/v1/boards/{token}/jobs?content=true` returns full postings with no authentication, and the single-job endpoint with `?questions=true` returns **the complete application form schema**: field names, the types `input_file`, `input_text`, `textarea` and `multi_value_single_select`, the options, plus EEOC and demographic blocks ([Greenhouse Job Board API](https://docs.greenhouse.io/job-board.html)). Lever's `api.lever.co/v0/postings/{site}?mode=json` and Ashby's `posting-api/job-board/{name}` do the same for listings ([lever/postings-api](https://github.com/lever/postings-api); [Ashby](https://developers.ashbyhq.com/docs/public-job-posting-api)). Ashby's `jobPosting.info` also returns form specs ([Ashby](https://developers.ashbyhq.com/docs/creating-a-custom-careers-page)). The catch is that every vendor's *submission* API requires the employer's key, so a third-party agent must still drive the hosted web form in a browser ([Greenhouse](https://docs.greenhouse.io/job-board.html); [Ashby applicationForm.submit](https://developers.ashbyhq.com/reference/applicationformsubmit)). The real maintenance burden is the slug list. Slugs are the path segment of the careers URL, they often differ from the company name, and they 404 when a company switches ATS. Searching the Common Crawl URL index for each ATS's host pattern found about 1,990 boards in one run ([Apify ats-company-discovery](https://apify.com/wickfeed/ats-company-discovery.md)). Paid aggregators cover the long tail at roughly **$1.50–$9 per 1,000 jobs** (vendor pricing) ([Bright Data](https://brightdata.com/blog/web-data/best-job-apis); [JobsPipe](https://jobspipe.dev/blog/theirstack-alternatives)). JobSpy scrapes LinkedIn and Indeed with one `pip install`, but LinkedIn returns 429 errors on bursts, and scraping it brings in the contract risk discussed below ([JobSpy](https://github.com/speedyapply/JobSpy)).

The apply engine works best in layers (inferred). Each layer is grounded in what the ATSs actually do. For Greenhouse, Lever and Ashby, fetch the form schema from the JSON endpoint, have the LLM answer the custom questions *before* opening a browser, then fill the real DOM so the reCAPTCHA Enterprise token and fingerprint stay valid. A third-party write-up found that replaying the Greenhouse POST outside the page was rejected at the captcha layer every time it tried. The same write-up lists several traps. Comboboxes are react-aria widgets, not native `<select>` elements. Location must be *clicked* from the geocode dropdown or latitude and longitude stay empty and submit fails silently. The email field has a roughly 500 ms debounced validator. Success shows up as `?application_id=` in the URL ([browse.sh Greenhouse skill](https://browse.sh/skills/greenhouse.com/apply-to-jobs-dsmal1), unofficial). Workday is the expensive case. Each employer runs its own instance and requires a separate candidate account, the resume parser mangles tables, and a manual application takes 20–30 minutes ([dev.to FastApply](https://dev.to/fastapply/how-to-beat-workday-applications-in-2026-the-ats-everyone-hates-and-how-to-auto-fill-it-36n6), vendor). Its `data-automation-id` attributes give stable selectors, so a dedicated script is feasible ([Nexthink](https://docs.nexthink.com/platform/user-guide/adopt/guide-creation-and-management-from-nexthink-applications/configuring-adoption-settings/use-case-element-selectors-for-workday)). iCIMS, Taleo, SuccessFactors and custom sites go to the generic agent. Stagehand's `observe()` returns a replayable selector, and with `selfHeal` on it re-infers the action once if that selector breaks, so a second application to the same employer costs close to zero tokens ([Stagehand act docs](https://docs.stagehand.dev/v3/basics/act)). The key design choice is to call the LLM **once per page to produce a fill plan**, a JSON object of `{field_id: {value, confidence, source, needs_review}}`, rather than once per keystroke. Deterministic code then executes the plan. That cuts an agent's 30–60 steps down to one to three LLM calls (inferred).

Several fields need hard rules, not LLM judgment. Work authorization, sponsorship, salary and legal attestations always force `needs_review`. EEO answers come only from the user's explicit stored choice and default to "decline to self-identify." Dropdowns are matched by fuzzy text first, then by an LLM limited to the options on offer. Never type free text into a combobox without selecting an option (inferred, based on the Greenhouse location behavior above). Every application should keep an evidence bundle: final screenshot, confirmation DOM, the answer JSON and the files used. Never auto-retry a submit until you have confirmed no earlier submission exists. AIHawk already logs a screenshot per page and checks that the posting is still open before applying ([AIHawk](https://github.com/feder-cr/Jobs_Applier_AI_Agent_AIHawk)).

## Build it in five phases, from a weekend script to a product

The plan below starts from the tightest legal and technical footing: local, reviewed, career pages only. It relaxes those limits only once there is evidence and infrastructure to support it. The phase boundaries are my recommendation. The parts they rely on are cited above.

| Phase | Scope | Exit criterion |
|---|---|---|
| 0. Data (1 weekend) | Python + Postgres (pgvector). Seed 200–500 target-company slugs by hand or from Common Crawl; hourly poller for Greenhouse, Lever and Ashby; dedupe on (vendor, slug, external_id); mark a job closed when it disappears from the feed | Fresh feed of relevant jobs posted in the last 24h |
| 1. Profile + match (1 weekend) | PDF to JSON Resume with bullet IDs (Pydantic-validated); hard filters; hybrid retrieval; LLM re-rank of the top 20 with matched requirements cited by bullet ID | Daily ranked digest you agree with |
| 2. Tailor (1–2 weeks) | Fork or reuse Resume-Matcher prompts; add the programmatic fact check (every number, proper noun and skill must trace to a source bullet) plus an LLM entailment judge; RenderCV output; text re-extraction check; show a diff | Zero unsupported claims on a 30-job eval set |
| 3. Apply, local (2–4 weeks) | Playwright with a dedicated Chrome profile on your own machine; Greenhouse, Lever and Ashby scripts built from the JSON schemas; fill plan from one LLM call; always stop before submit for you to approve; state machine saved after each step | Over 90% clean fills on those three ATSs; you click submit |
| 4. Track + widen (2–4 weeks) | Gmail watch on your own account; email classifier; Workday script with per-employer credentials in the OS keychain; Stagehand fallback for the long tail; saved Q&A memory reused across applications | Outcome dashboard: applications → replies → interviews |
| 5. Product (months) | Multi-tenant cloud workers or an extension-first design; KMS-encrypted per-tenant vault; per-ATS rate limits; billing; CASA; ToS, consent and do-not-fabricate controls | See risk section |

The Phase 3 setup needs care. **Since Chrome 136, `--remote-debugging-port` is ignored on the default Chrome profile**, a change made because infostealers abused it. It still blocks access as of Chrome 151 ([Chrome developer blog](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/remote-debugging-port); [trycua issue #2916](https://github.com/trycua/cua/issues/2916)). A local tool therefore launches Chrome with its own `--user-data-dir` and attaches with `connectOverCDP`. You log into employer sites once in that profile. Playwright's `setInputFiles` works on hidden file inputs. If you would rather build a Chrome extension, content scripts run in your real logged-in session. That gives the best detection profile and is what top-rated Simplify uses. You must build file uploads with `DataTransfer` and set `all_frames` to reach iframes (inferred).

For Phase 4, Workday is where account custody becomes unavoidable. You need one generated password per employer, email verification read from your inbox, and should expect captchas at account creation (inferred). This is also where a coding agent can help. ApplyPilot (Feb 2026) has Claude Code drive the browser on arbitrary ATS forms, but it is blocked by CAPTCHAs unless you add a CapSolver key, and one low-quality report has Claude Haiku resetting a LinkedIn password partway through an application ([SourcePulse ApplyPilot](https://www.sourcepulse.org/projects/24998881); [aggregator blog](https://ai-tools-aggregator-seven.vercel.app/blog/2026-02-19-applypilot-claude-haiku-job-agent)). Generic agents need a domain allowlist, a ban on account-security actions, and a rule to stop at any unknown question. Anthropic's own computer-use guidance says the same: use an isolated VM or container, keep credentials away from the model, and have a human confirm consequential actions ([Anthropic computer use docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/agents-and-tools/tool-use/computer-use-tool)).

For Phase 5, Tsenta's own evolution is the warning. Moving to the cloud buys convenience and creates every hard problem at once. Cloud browsers need stealth, residential proxies and captcha handling. Browserbase's Developer tier is $20/month with basic stealth and captcha solving, and advanced stealth is only on custom-priced plans ([tinyfish](https://www.tinyfish.ai/blog/browserbase-pricing), third-party). Greenhouse reportedly flags data-center IPs, VPNs and proxies through IPQualityScore and checks email age ([Jobscan](https://www.jobscan.co/blog/auto-apply-job-tools/), secondhand). Publishing a Gmail-reading app with `gmail.readonly`, a restricted scope, requires a **CASA assessment by a Google-authorized lab, renewed every 12 months** ([EmailEngine](https://learn.emailengine.app/docs/accounts/gmail/gmail-api-scopes)). Tsenta started out routing mail through Composio instead ([Privacy](https://tsenta.com/privacy)). Two cheaper options are an address you control (`you+apply@yourdomain`) used as the application email, or narrower scopes (inferred, to evaluate). If you go desktop, macOS Sequoia removed the Control-click override for unnotarized apps, so you need Developer ID signing and notarization. On Windows, Azure Artifact Signing costs about $9.99/month, and **EV certificates no longer bypass SmartScreen** ([MacRumors](https://macrumors.com/2024/08/06/macos-sequoia-gatekeeper-security-change); [Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/windows/apps/package-and-deploy/code-signing-options)).

## Applications cost cents in tokens, but churn sets the business ceiling

Per-application cost depends almost entirely on whether the agent reads structure or pixels. Using cached Anthropic list prices per 1M tokens of **Haiku 5.5 $0.10/$0.50, Sonnet 5.5 $2/$10, Opus 5.5 $4/$20** (verify live at [platform.claude.com pricing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing)), the token estimates below are mine. They are not measured from a real agent.

| Path | Tokens per application (est.) | LLM cost per application | Time (est.) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Schema-first script + one fill-plan call (Greenhouse/Lever/Ashby) | 10–30K | under $0.01 (Haiku) to ~$0.10 (Sonnet) | 1–2 min |
| Accessibility-tree agent, ~80K in / 5K out | 40–120K in | ~$0.01 Haiku, ~$0.21 Sonnet, ~$0.42 Opus, before caching | 1–3 min |
| browser-use cloud, 10-step task | n/a | ~$0.03 ($0.002/step + $0.01 init) ([browser-use pricing](https://docs.cloud.browser-use.com/pricing)) | ~3 s/step ([browser-use](https://browser-use.com/posts/speed-matters)) |
| Vision computer-use on a Workday wizard | 300K–1M+ (1–1.8K per screenshot) ([Anthropic](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/agents-and-tools/tool-use/computer-use-tool)) | ~$0.60–$2 on Sonnet-class | 5–15 min |
| Resume tailoring add-on | +5–10K | a few tenths of a cent to a few cents | seconds |

The figures agree with the few published data points. AIHawk says each application costs "a few cents" in LLM credit ([AIHawk](https://github.com/feder-cr/Jobs_Applier_AI_Agent_AIHawk)). Tsenta's two-to-three-cent price works only if most of its traffic takes the first row. Browser time is cheap: at about five minutes per application and $0.10–$0.12 per browser-hour, it comes to roughly a cent ([tinyfish](https://www.tinyfish.ai/blog/browserbase-pricing)). Residential proxies, captcha solving and failed runs make up the hidden cloud costs. Because Tsenta charges only for successful submissions, it absorbs the cost of failed runs, and that rewards reliable confirmation detection (inferred).

For **personal use**, total monthly cost is tiny. The ATS feeds are free, Postgres runs locally, and 300 applications on a Haiku-with-Sonnet-for-free-text pipeline costs roughly $5–$15. A heavy all-Sonnet run costs about $63 uncached (my estimate from [list prices](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing)). An optional aggregator starts around $59/month ([JobsPipe](https://jobspipe.dev/blog/theirstack-alternatives)). Running locally means no browser bill. For a **product**, the per-user cost of goods holds up at $30–$60 pricing, provided you route models in a hybrid pipeline. The problem is lifetime value. Job seekers churn by design once hired, and consumer subscription apps lose over half their monthly subscribers within three months ([Phil Carter via Recall](https://www.recall.it/summary/startup/phil-carter-growth-loops-cac-ltv-benchmarks-pricing-discounts-paywalls-or-e1204)). At 15–30% monthly churn and $30 average revenue per user, gross lifetime value lands around $100–$200 (inferred), which leaves little room for paid acquisition. Fixed product costs include CASA every year, an Apple developer membership (commonly $99/year, unverified), Windows signing at ~$120/year, and SOC 2 only if you sell to universities or outplacement firms.

Volume does not get people hired, so the economics should not push you toward volume. Jobscan puts callback rates at roughly **1–6% for fully automated tools versus 5–15% for copilots** where a human reviews ([Jobscan](https://www.jobscan.co/blog/auto-apply-job-tools/), a vendor with a competing product). One AIHawk user sent 2,843 applications for 4 interviews ([404 Media](https://404media.co/i-applied-to-2-843-roles-the-rise-of-ai-powered-job-application-bots)). A secondhand Reddit post describes 5,000 LazyApply applications producing the same 20 interviews as 200 manual ones ([Shortlisted](https://shortlisted.site/blog/auto-apply-bots-dont-work)). Meanwhile applications per hire rose from about 100 in 2021 to over 300 in 2025 ([Jobscan citing Ashby](https://www.jobscan.co/blog/auto-apply-job-tools/)), and Greenhouse's CEO calls the result an "AI doom loop" ([Greenhouse](https://www.greenhouse.com/newsroom/an-ai-trust-crisis-70-of-hiring-managers-trust-ai-to-make-faster-and-better-hiring-decisions-only-8-of-job-seekers-call-it-fair)). Set your tool to apply less often, to better-matched roles, with truthful tailoring, and log interview yield, not applications sent.

## Contract enforcement and employer detection outweigh criminal law

The likely harms run in this order: **account bans and blocking first, cease-and-desist letters second, contract suits third, and CFAA claims last** (ranking inferred from the cases below). This is not legal advice. Several points are unsettled.

Contract claims carry the real legal force. In *hiQ v. LinkedIn*, the court enforced LinkedIn's anti-scraping and anti-fake-profile terms as a contract, and the case ended with a **$500,000 judgment, a permanent injunction, and an order to delete scraped data and code** ([Proskauer](https://www.proskauer.com/blog/hiq-and-linkedin-reach-proposed-settlement-in-landmark-scraping-case); [Privacy World](https://www.privacyworld.blog/2022/12/linkedins-data-scraping-battle-with-hiq-labs-ends-with-proposed-judgment/)). LinkedIn's current User Agreement bans "bots or other unauthorized automated methods" (8.2.13), "browser plugins and add-ons" that scrape (8.2.2), and sharing log-ins or "copying cookies" (8.2.1). The last clause hits any cloud agent that holds a LinkedIn session ([LinkedIn User Agreement](https://www.linkedin.com/legal/user-agreement)). LinkedIn also now sells its own Premium Apply Assistant, which gives it a commercial reason to enforce ([Let's Data Science](https://letsdatascience.com/news/linkedin-launches-premium-apply-assistant-to-autofill-applic-5adb3af3)). It has enforced against open source too: AIHawk's README says LinkedIn "banned all project contributors," and the project dropped LinkedIn automation ([feder-cr README](https://github.com/feder-cr/linkedIn_auto_jobs_applier_with_AI/blob/main/README.md)). *Meta v. Bright Data* helps only for logged-out discovery, because an agent applying while logged in is a "user" bound by the terms ([Farella Braun](https://www.fbm.com/technology/publications/major-decision-affects-law-of-scraping-and-online-data-collection-meta-platforms-v-bright-data/)). On the CFAA, *Van Buren* narrowed the statute to a test of whether access was authorized at all, and in August 2026 the Ninth Circuit in *Amazon v. Perplexity* held that **a user-directed AI agent is not a "whoever" under the CFAA; the user is the one accessing the site**. The court left open whether more developer control changes that, and noted that Amazon can still enforce its ToS ([ZwillGen](https://www.zwillgen.com/artificial-intelligence/web-scraping-digest-9th-circuit-addresses-agentic-ai/)). Other circuits have not ruled. Workday's terms reportedly bar scraping and interfering with the site ([Conduct Atlas](https://conductatlas.com/platform/workday/workday-terms-of-use/provision/CA-P-076032/prohibition-on-scraping-or-data-extraction/), third-party), and at least one auto-apply service excludes Workday sites for that reason ([4dayweek.io](https://4dayweek.io/acceptable-use)).

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| LinkedIn/Indeed bans, contract suits | Do not automate LinkedIn; at most deep-link to it. Use ATS feeds or licensed aggregators for discovery, and apply on employer career pages. |
| ATS bot/fraud detection (Greenhouse Real Talent; IP, email-age and CLEAR checks) ([Greenhouse](https://www.greenhouse.com/newsroom/greenhouse-real-talent-tm-launches-to-fix-overwhelming-candidate-pipelines-while-combatting-fraud-and-spam-in-hiring)) | Run locally on your residential IP and real browser, apply at human pace with a low daily cap, do not solve CAPTCHAs (hand them to the user), and do not spoof proxies |
| False statements and fabricated answers (a JobCopilot user reports an invented name, gender and reference) ([Trustpilot](https://uk.trustpilot.com/review/jobcopilot.com)) | Factual answers come only from a user-verified profile; unknown answers stop the run; programmatic fact check on tailoring; no "Aggressive" mode; a do-not-fabricate eval suite |
| Work-authorization and EEO attestations | Only the user sets these, never the LLM; EEO defaults to decline; under GDPR these are special-category data that need explicit consent (inferred) |
| Credential custody | Personal: OS keychain. Product: KMS envelope encryption, per-user keys, access logs |
| Agency and consent | Clickwrap terms appointing the service as the user's limited agent; per-application approval log with a snapshot of what was submitted (inferred; check with counsel) |
| Privacy and LLM data use | Zero-data-retention-eligible stateless APIs (some newer Anthropic features are not ZDR-eligible) ([platform.claude.com](https://platform.claude.com/docs)); short retention; no training on user data without opt-in |
| AI regulation | A candidate-side tool is probably outside EU AI Act Annex III(4), whose high-risk obligations are delayed to Dec 2, 2027 ([Sidley](https://datamatters.sidley.com/2026/06/22/eu-lawmakers-reach-provisional-agreement-to-delay-key-eu-ai-act-obligations/)). Art. 50 transparency applies from Aug 2, 2026 ([Airia](https://airia.com/the-eu-ai-act-just-got-an-extension-heres-how-to-use-it/)). NYC LL144 binds employers, not candidates ([Vaquill](https://www.vaquill.ai/blog/nyc-local-law-144-automated-hiring-explained)). Building an employer-facing feed, which Tsenta says it plans, would pull you into scope |
| Vendor continuity | Sonara shut down abruptly in Feb 2024 and users lost their history ([LoopCV](https://blog.loopcv.pro/what-happened-to-sonara/)); offer export and keep your own records |

The table also shows a tension in Tsenta's design. Each step it took toward the cloud, convenience and Auto Apply moves away from the safest posture the law currently supports: the user's own session, on the user's own device, with a human approving each submission. The Perplexity reasoning favors a tool the user directs. Custody of credentials and cookies conflicts with LinkedIn's terms. Data-center IPs are what ATS fraud systems look for. A personal build can take the safe position at no cost. A product has to decide how much of it to give up for the sake of convenience.

## Conclusion

Discovery, parsing and tailoring are now cheap and mostly commodity. Tsenta's real advantage sits in the parts nobody publishes: maintained per-ATS scripts, reliable confirmation detection, Workday account handling, and whatever it does about captchas and IPs at cloud scale. A two-person team reached millions in claimed revenue in a few months, which shows the demand. The two-to-three-cent price shows the engineering bar: fixed per-site scripts plus one LLM fill plan per form, with LLM agents only as a fallback. Copy the review gates and the career-page-only stance. Skip the "Aggressive" tailoring mode and the 150-a-day Auto Apply.

For your own use, the best result is not a smaller Tsenta. It is a better-targeted one: local, reviewed, selective, and measured by interviews. That costs a few dollars a month and avoids most of the platform risk. Scaling it into a product is mainly a change of risk and economics, not of technology. You take on credential custody, CASA, bot-detection work and a customer base that churns once it succeeds. That makes a B2B2C channel through universities and outplacement firms, and possibly the employer side Tsenta says it will build next, more durable than a consumer volume subscription.
